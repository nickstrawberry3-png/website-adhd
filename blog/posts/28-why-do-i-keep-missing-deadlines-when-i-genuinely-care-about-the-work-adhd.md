# Why do I keep missing deadlines when I genuinely care about the work?

> **Quick answer:** Because caring is not what makes a deadline work. Proximity is. A deadline three weeks out produces no pull at all and then produces all of it at once, which is why work that matters to you is often the latest. The fix is interim dates given to a real person.


The painful part of this is what it looks like from outside. Missing a deadline reads as not caring. For most of the people we work with it is the opposite: the work they care about most is the work most likely to be late.

A videographer we spoke to had a wedding video from the previous June still unfinished. He was free to do it. He described feeling like he was lying to the couple. He cared enormously, and that was part of what made it harder.

## Why doesn't caring help?

Because caring operates on the outcome and deadlines operate on the next few hours.

What produces action is proximity. A thing due today pulls. A thing due in three weeks does not pull at all, and the difference between them is not importance, it is distance.

So a three-week deadline produces nineteen days of nothing and two days of panic. Which is why the work gets done, late, at enormous cost.

## Why does caring sometimes make it worse?

Two reasons.

**The standard goes up.** Work you care about carries an expectation you will do it well, which makes starting riskier, because starting means finding out.

**Guilt accumulates.** Once something is late, looking at it means facing the lateness. So we avoid the task to avoid the feeling, which makes it later, which makes the feeling worse.

That is how a two-week delay becomes a year. Not through indifference. Through the delay becoming its own obstacle.

## What actually fixes it?

**Interim dates, given to a person.** Not milestones in your own plan. Dates you have told the client or a colleague, with something small due on each one. A first cut on the 8th. A draft on the 15th. Each one is close enough to pull.

**Make the first date very soon.** Within a few days of starting. The first deliverable should be small enough to be almost embarrassing. Its job is to start the relationship with the work, not to impress.

**Reply before you are ready.** The biggest cause of a late project becoming an abandoned one is the silence around it. A one-line update on the 8th keeps it alive even if nothing is attached.

**Give the finishing work its own slot.** The last twenty percent has no novelty in it, which is where most things stall. Schedule it separately and treat it as a different task.

| | One final deadline | Interim dates |
|---|---|---|
| Pull in week one | None | Real |
| What happens | Nothing, then panic | Steady small progress |
| Cost | Crash afterwards | Minimal |
| If you slip | The whole thing is late | One small date is late |

## What do I do about the ones already late?

Send something today. Not the finished work, a message.

Short, no long explanation, with a date attached that you have thought about. Most people respond normally. The version in your head where they are furious is almost never the version that happens.

Then do the easiest late item first, not the oldest. The point of the first one is to prove to yourself that these can be finished.

## How do I stop it happening again?

The pattern returns whenever a project has one date far away and nobody checking in between. So the structural fix is to never have that shape again.

Every project gets interim dates and a person. That is the whole prevention.

## FAQ

**Why can I hit other people's deadlines but not my own?**
Other people's deadlines have a person attached. Your own do not, and a deadline with nobody waiting is a preference.

**Is it better to under-promise on dates?**
Yes, substantially. Most people with this pattern quote the date that assumes everything goes perfectly. Quote the realistic one and deliver early occasionally.

**Should I tell clients I am running late before the deadline?**
Always, and as early as possible. Early notice is treated completely differently from silence followed by a missed date.

**What if the project is so late I cannot face it?**
Send one line today with no commitment attached. The silence is doing more damage than the delay.
