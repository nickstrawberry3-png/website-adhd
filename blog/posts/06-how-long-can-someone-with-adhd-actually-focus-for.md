# How long can someone with ADHD actually focus for?

> **Quick answer:** There is no fixed number, and the honest figure for most adults with ADHD is shorter than the hour that productivity advice assumes. We call it the Drift Point: the minutes before attention starts to go. For many people it is fifteen to twenty-five minutes on ordinary work, and hours on something interesting.


Most people ask this question because they have failed at a system built on a number that was never theirs.

Pomodoro says twenty-five. Deep work says ninety. Time blocking says an hour. If your real figure is twelve minutes on a boring task, all three will fail, and you will assume you are the problem.

## What is the Drift Point?

The number of minutes you can stay on a task before attention starts to move away from it.

It is not the point where you give up. It is the earlier moment where you notice you have stopped reading the sentence, or your hand has gone to your phone, or you are thinking about something else.

It matters because it is the correct length for a work block. A twenty-minute block you finish beats a sixty-minute block you abandon at minute nine.

## Why is it different for every task?

Because ADHD attention is driven by interest, not duration.

The same person can lose focus in eight minutes on a spreadsheet and work four hours straight on something they find absorbing. That is not inconsistency. It is the same mechanism producing two different results depending on the input.

One of our clients, a videographer, could shoot all day without effort and could not sit through editing at all. Same brain, same day, same work. Shooting had novelty. Editing did not.

So the useful thing is not one Drift Point. It is your Drift Point on the tasks you avoid, because those are the ones that need a block built around them.

## How do I measure mine?

Pick a task you normally avoid. Start a timer. Work until you notice attention moving. Stop the timer and write the number. Do it three or four times across different days.

Take the lowest honest number, not the best one. The block has to work on an ordinary day, not your best day.

| Type of task | Typical Drift Point |
|---|---|
| Work you find interesting | Long, sometimes hours |
| Routine work you do not mind | Moderate |
| Admin, forms, anything repetitive | Often under twenty minutes |
| Work that carries guilt attached | Shortest of all |

## What do I do with the number?

Build blocks that length, then stop on purpose.

Stopping while you still have attention left is the part people skip, and it is the part that makes it repeatable. If you run every block until you are drained, the next block is harder, and by the afternoon there are none left.

Three rules that make it hold:

1. One task per block, decided before you start
2. Stop at the timer even if you feel fine, at least for the first two weeks
3. If you are in flow and genuinely want to continue, continue, but treat that as the exception

## Does the Drift Point get longer?

Usually yes, for two reasons. Sleep and energy improve it directly. And finishing blocks builds the expectation that a block ends in success rather than abandonment, which lowers the resistance to starting the next one.

What does not lengthen it is pushing through. Pushing through produces a longer session today and a shorter one tomorrow.

## FAQ

**Is the ADHD attention span really shorter?**
On tasks that are not interesting, usually yes. On tasks that are, often much longer than average. Averaging the two produces a number that describes nobody.

**Should I use Pomodoro?**
Only if twenty-five minutes happens to be near your real number. If it is not, use your number instead of the one on the app.

**What if my Drift Point is five minutes?**
Then work in five-minute blocks. Five minutes repeated is a large amount of work. Nothing repeated is not.

**Does hyperfocus mean I don't have ADHD?**
No. Long absorbed focus on interesting things is common with ADHD, and it is the same mechanism that makes boring work so hard.
