# How much sleep do people with ADHD need, and why is it so hard to get?

> **Quick answer:** The requirement is the same as anyone else, around seven to nine hours for most adults. Getting it is harder, because delayed sleep timing is very common with ADHD, and the evening is often the first time all day the mind is quiet. Consistency of wake time matters more than total hours.


This is general information and not medical advice. Persistent sleep problems are worth taking to your own doctor, because several treatable conditions look like this.

Sleep is the first thing we look at with every client, before any system, app or routine. It is also the thing people most want to skip past.

## Why is sleep the first thing to fix?

Because every tool downstream of it runs on energy, and energy comes from sleep.

A person running on five hours cannot execute a prioritisation system. They can learn it, agree with it and write it down. They cannot run it on Thursday afternoon. The system then gets blamed, and the search for a better system begins.

That is the reason the Fuel stage comes first. Energy first. Systems second.

## Why do I stay up when I am exhausted?

Several reasons show up again and again.

**The evening is the first quiet time.** For people who spend the day managing interruptions, the hours after everyone is asleep are the first hours that belong to them. Giving them up feels like a loss, because it is one.

**Stopping requires a decision.** Going to bed means stopping something, and stopping is a transition. Transitions are hard, so we stay where we are.

**The phone.** Scrolling in bed pushes sleep later without ever feeling like a choice.

**Delayed sleep timing.** Many people with ADHD genuinely do not feel sleepy until late. That part is not a discipline problem.

## What should I actually change?

One thing, and it is not bedtime.

**Fix the wake time.** The same time every day, including weekends, for two weeks. Bedtime follows wake time, not the other way round. Trying to go to bed earlier when you are not tired produces an hour of lying awake and a worse association with bed.

Then three supports:

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| Daylight within an hour of waking | Anchors the body clock more strongly than anything else available |
| Phone out of the bedroom | Removes both the scrolling and the waking checks |
| A fixed stop time for work | Gives the evening a boundary, so quiet time is deliberate rather than stolen |

## How much sleep do I actually need?

The usual adult range applies, around seven to nine hours. ADHD does not change the requirement, it changes how easy it is to meet.

What is worth noticing is the gap between time in bed and time asleep. One client was in bed for seven hours and estimated real sleep considerably lower, because of scrolling before sleep. The fix there was not more hours. It was the phone.

## When should I see a doctor about this?

Worth raising with your doctor if:

- You sleep a full night and wake unrefreshed most days
- You snore heavily or someone has noticed you stop breathing
- You have restless or uncomfortable legs in the evening
- Sleep problems started or changed when your medication changed
- Nothing here helps after a genuine month of trying

Sleep apnoea, restless legs and thyroid problems all cause symptoms that look like worsening ADHD, and all are treatable.

## FAQ

**Does ADHD medication cause insomnia?**
It can, particularly taken later in the day. Timing is something to discuss with your prescriber rather than adjust yourself.

**Is it true people with ADHD are night owls?**
Delayed sleep timing is more common in ADHD than in the general population. It is a real pattern, not a preference, though it can shift with consistent light and wake times.

**Should I nap?**
A short early-afternoon nap suits some people. Long or late naps usually make the night worse.

**What if my job means irregular hours?**
Then anchor what you can. Shift workers get less from a fixed wake time and more from controlling light exposure and protecting the longest sleep block.
