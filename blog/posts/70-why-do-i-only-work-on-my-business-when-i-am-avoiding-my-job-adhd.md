# Why do I only work on my business when I am avoiding my job?

> **Quick answer:** Because avoidance supplies energy. Anything is more attractive than the task you are dodging, so the side project gets your attention precisely when something else is more unpleasant. It feels like motivation and it is not, which is why it disappears the moment the job gets quiet.


People notice this and feel vaguely dishonest about it. They are most productive on their own thing during the busiest week at work, and do nothing on a quiet one.

It is not a character problem. It is a predictable effect, and understanding it tells you something useful about how to get the work done.

## What is actually happening?

The side project is not winning on its own merits. It is winning because it is not the thing you are avoiding.

Anything becomes attractive when placed next to a task you are dodging. This is why people deep clean the kitchen before an exam, and why the business plan gets written during the week the report is due.

So the energy is real, and it is borrowed. It belongs to the avoidance, not to the project.

## Why does that matter?

Because it tells you what the side project is missing.

On a quiet week at work, with nothing to avoid, the project gets nothing. That is the test, and it shows that the project has no pull of its own. It only ever moves when something else pushes.

Which means the fix is not to find more motivation. It is to give the project the same thing the job has: a person, a date, and a consequence you can feel this week.

| | Your job | Your business | What to add |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deadline | Real | None | A date told to someone |
| Someone expecting it | Yes | No | One person, weekly |
| Consequence | Immediate | None | Reporting what you did |
| When you work on it | Always | When avoiding something | A fixed slot |

## Is there a problem with using it anyway?

Two, and they are worth knowing.

**It is unreliable.** The work only happens when the job is unpleasant, which you do not control.

**It damages the job.** The report that was being avoided is still due, and this pattern tends to end with both things late. That is the version that causes real trouble.

So it is better treated as information about what is missing than as a strategy.

## What to do instead

**Give the project a fixed slot.** Same time weekly, in good hours, not in stolen ones.

**Tell one person what you will have done by Friday.** This is the whole fix in one sentence. The project needs the thing your job already has.

**Deal with the avoided task separately.** Usually it needs a Step Zero, a slot and the same person. Otherwise it stays as the engine of this pattern.

**Notice when it happens.** If you find yourself unusually productive on the business, ask what you are avoiding. The answer is usually immediate, and often the more important thing to do.

## FAQ

**Is this procrastination or productivity?**
Both, which is why it is confusing. Real work gets done, on the wrong thing, at the wrong time.

**Should I feel guilty about it?**
Guilt does not change it. The useful response is to notice what is being avoided and deal with that.

**What if my job is genuinely unbearable?**
Then that is a separate and bigger question, and worth facing directly rather than through a side project that only moves when work is bad.

**Will the business work if I leave the job?**
Not automatically, and this pattern is a warning sign. If the project only moves under avoidance pressure, it will need real structure once the job is gone.
