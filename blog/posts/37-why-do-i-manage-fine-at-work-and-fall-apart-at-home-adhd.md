# Why do I manage fine at work and fall apart at home?

> **Quick answer:** Because work supplies the structure and home does not. At work there are deadlines, colleagues and consequences you can feel today. At home nobody is waiting, nothing is urgent, and the same brain that performed all day cannot start the dishes. It is not inconsistency, it is the absence of external pull.


This one surprises people, because it does not match what they expect ADHD to look like. They hold down a demanding job, often a senior one, and then cannot deal with a letter that has been on the table for three weeks.

Several of the people we work with describe exactly this split. Thriving at work, stuck at home.

## Why is home harder than work?

Because every structural support disappears at the front door.

| | At work | At home |
|---|---|---|
| Someone expecting things | Constantly | Nobody |
| Deadlines | Real and dated | None, or self-set |
| Consequence of not doing it | Visible, soon | Invisible for months |
| Variety | High | The same chores, repeatedly |
| Being observed | Yes | No |

Every one of those is a trigger an ADHD brain uses to start. Work supplies all five. Home supplies none, so the same person performs completely differently in the two places.

There is a second factor. Work has already used the day's capacity. By the time you get home, the resource the chores need has been spent.

## Is this still ADHD if work is fine?

Yes, and this pattern is one of the reasons people get diagnosed late. The structured environments, school, university, employment, carried them. The diagnosis often follows the first time the structure goes away, when they go self-employed, work from home, or have a period of leave.

It also means the common self-test of am I coping is misleading. Coping at work says more about the workplace than about you.

## What actually helps at home?

**Give the chores an external pull.** This is the whole problem, so it is the whole answer. Anything that makes someone else aware works better than any system. A friend you message, a weekly call, a cleaner who comes on Thursdays so the tidying has a deadline.

**Make it a fixed slot, not whenever you get to it.** Same time each week, attached to something that already happens. Decided once rather than daily.

**Shrink it to Step Zero.** Not do the washing. Put the washing in the machine. Not sort the paperwork. Open the envelope on top.

**Do not spend your best hours at work and expect home to get anything.** If something at home genuinely matters, give it a weekend morning rather than a Tuesday evening.

**Lower the standard deliberately.** A half-done kitchen beats an untouched one, and most people in this pattern have an all-or-nothing rule that guarantees nothing.

## What about the effect on other people?

This is the part people find hardest to talk about. Partners and family see the version of you that has nothing left, and the fair question of why you can do it at work and not here is almost impossible to answer well in the moment.

What helps is naming the mechanism rather than defending yourself. Work is holding me up and home has nothing holding me up is both true and more useful than an apology. Then put something in place, because explanations without changes wear out quickly.

## FAQ

**Does this mean my ADHD is mild?**
Not necessarily. It means your work environment is doing a lot of the management for you, which is a different thing.

**Why can I run a team and not do my own washing?**
The team has people waiting. The washing does not. That single difference explains most of this.

**What happens if I change jobs or go self-employed?**
This is the most common point at which people suddenly struggle, because the structure that was carrying them goes away. Worth building your own before you need it.

**Should I get assessed if work is going well?**
If home is consistently difficult in the way described here, it is worth a conversation with your doctor. Coping in one domain does not rule anything out.
