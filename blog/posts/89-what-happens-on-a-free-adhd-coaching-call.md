# What happens on a free ADHD coaching call?

> **Quick answer:** On ours, about thirty to forty-five minutes. We work out what you are actually stuck on, what you have already tried, and what you want to be different. You get ideas you can use either way. If we can help, we say how at the end. If we cannot, we point you somewhere better.


Most people booking a free call are braced for a sales pitch with a consultation attached. That is a reasonable thing to expect, so here is what actually happens on ours.

## What the call is for

Finding out what is actually wrong, which is usually not what people arrive thinking.

Someone books saying they cannot focus. Twenty minutes in it turns out they sleep five hours, spend their sharpest three hours on other people's problems, and have nobody expecting anything of them. Focus was the symptom.

So the call is mostly questions. Not a quiz, and the questions are specific: what did you not do this week, what happened when you sat down to start, what have you already tried and what happened to it.

## The shape of it

**What is going on right now.** The actual week, not a summary. Specific examples work better than general descriptions.

**What it is costing.** Money, time, standing at work, evenings and weekends. This part is uncomfortable and it is the part that makes the rest useful.

**What you have already tried.** Most people have tried a great deal, and knowing what failed and how tells us more than anything else.

**What you want to be different.** Specific and dated where possible. Not be more productive. Send the invoices, launch the thing, get the evenings back.

**Our read on it.** Which part is actually broken and what we would do about it.

**Whether we can help.** If yes, we explain how and what it costs. If no, we say so and suggest what would be better.

## What it is not

**Not a diagnosis.** A coach cannot diagnose ADHD and should not try. If you want an assessment, that is your doctor.

**Not therapy.** If what comes up is depression, anxiety or something unresolved from the past, we will say that a therapist is the better place to start. That happens on a reasonable number of calls.

**Not a pressure call.** You will hear what it costs at the end if it is relevant. You will not be pushed to decide on the call, and nobody should be buying coaching on a deadline.

## How to get the most out of it

| Before the call | Why |
|---|---|
| Write down three things you did not do this week | Specific examples beat general descriptions |
| Think about what the problem has cost you | Makes the rest of the conversation concrete |
| List what you have already tried, and how it ended | The failures are the most useful information |
| Know what you want different in three months | Gives the plan something to aim at |

You do not need to prepare. People who do get more out of it.

## What if I am not sure I want coaching?

That is fine and it is most people. The call is useful on its own, and you will leave with something to try whether or not you go further.

If you already know you cannot afford it, say so at the start. We will keep the call useful and skip the part about the programme.

## FAQ

**Is it really free?**
Yes. No card, no commitment.

**How long is it?**
Thirty to forty-five minutes.

**Will I be pressured to buy?**
No. If something is relevant we will explain it at the end, once.

**What if I do not know what my problem is?**
That is common and it is what the call is for. You need examples, not a diagnosis.

**Do I need an ADHD diagnosis to book?**
No. A lot of the people we talk to are undiagnosed or waiting for an assessment.
