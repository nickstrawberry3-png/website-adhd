# Should I tell occupational health that I have ADHD?

> **Quick answer:** Often yes, and it is usually a better first conversation than your line manager, because occupational health can recommend adjustments without necessarily passing on the detail. Ask what they share before you tell them anything, and go in with a specific adjustment in mind rather than a diagnosis alone.


This is general information, not legal or medical advice. What occupational health can do, and what they pass to your employer, varies by country, by employer and sometimes by service. Ask before you disclose.

People usually ask this question when something has already gone wrong, which is the hardest moment to have the conversation well.

## What does occupational health actually do?

They assess whether you are fit for your role and recommend adjustments that would help. They are not there to discipline you, and in most systems they do not hand over your clinical detail.

What they typically pass to an employer is a recommendation, not a diagnosis. Something like a recommendation for protected administrative time, rather than the reason for it.

That distinction is the main reason this route is usually safer than telling a manager directly. But it varies, so the first question in the appointment should be what will be shared and with whom.

## When is it worth doing?

**Worth doing when you have a specific ask.** Protected documentation time. A more predictable rota. Written rather than verbal task handover. Extra time in an exam. Going in with the adjustment makes the conversation concrete and much more likely to produce something.

**Worth doing when something is already slipping.** Earlier is better. A referral you initiate reads completely differently from one triggered by an incident.

**Worth doing before an assessment or exam** where adjustments need to be in place in advance, because the paperwork usually takes longer than people expect.

## When is it worth more caution?

If you have no specific ask and simply want the diagnosis on record, think about what that achieves for you. Disclosure cannot be undone.

If your employer has a poor record on this, the calculation changes, and it is worth talking to a union representative or professional body first. They deal with this regularly and will know the local picture.

| | Worth it | Think first |
|---|---|---|
| You have a specific adjustment in mind | Yes | |
| Something is already slipping and you want it on record early | Yes | |
| You need adjustments for an exam | Yes | |
| No specific ask, just want it known | | Consider what it achieves |
| Employer has handled this badly before | | Speak to a union or professional body first |

## What should I ask in the appointment?

Five questions worth having ready:

1. What exactly will you pass to my employer, and what will you not
2. Will the diagnosis itself be named, or only the recommendations
3. Who in the organisation sees the report
4. Can I see the report before it goes
5. What adjustments have you seen work for people in my role

The fourth one matters more than people realise and is often available if you ask.

## What adjustments are worth requesting?

The ones that address the actual difficulty rather than the diagnosis. Protected time for documentation inside the shift. Non-urgent tasks given in writing rather than verbally. Predictable or forward-rotating rota patterns. A quieter space for administrative work. Regular short check-ins rather than long infrequent reviews.

Each of those is a reasonable operational request that happens to solve an ADHD problem.

## FAQ

**Will telling occupational health affect my registration?**
Under many healthcare regulators, a diagnosis alone does not trigger fitness to practise action unless it affects safe practice. Rules vary widely in other countries and in safety-critical industries, where disclosure of a diagnosis or a prescription is sometimes mandatory regardless of impairment. Check your own regulator's published guidance.

**Does my employer have to make adjustments?**
In many countries ADHD can qualify as a disability with a duty to make reasonable adjustments. The detail varies a great deal, so check locally.

**Can I ask for adjustments without disclosing?**
Often yes. Many useful changes, like written task handover, can be requested as a working preference without naming anything.

**Should I tell my manager as well?**
Separate decision. Start with occupational health, see what they recommend, then decide.
