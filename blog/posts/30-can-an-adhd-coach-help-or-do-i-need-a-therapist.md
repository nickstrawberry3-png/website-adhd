# Can an ADHD coach help, or do I need a therapist?

> **Quick answer:** They do different things. A therapist works on mental health, past experience and how you feel. A coach works on what you do this week: energy, systems and someone checking on you. If your main problem is depression, anxiety or trauma, start with a therapist. If your main problem is that you know what to do and cannot make yourself do it, that is coaching.


This is general information, not medical advice. If you are struggling with your mental health, your own doctor is the right first step.

We would rather tell someone to see a therapist than take money for work that is not going to help them, so here is the honest version.

## What is the actual difference?

| | Therapist | ADHD coach |
|---|---|---|
| Works on | Mental health, past experience, how you feel | What you do this week |
| Typical focus | Anxiety, depression, trauma, patterns of thinking | Sleep, energy, starting, finishing, accountability |
| Regulated | Yes, licensed | No, anyone can use the title |
| Can diagnose | Some can | No |
| Can prescribe | No, that is a doctor | No |
| Between sessions | Usually nothing | Usually check-ins |

The regulation row matters. Coaching is unregulated, which means the quality range is enormous. One of our clients described her previous coach as a waste of money, and she was not wrong to say so.

## When should I see a therapist first?

If any of these describe you, start there rather than with coaching:

- Persistent low mood, loss of interest in things you used to enjoy
- Anxiety that is affecting your sleep or your ability to function
- Past experiences that keep affecting the present
- Any thoughts of harming yourself
- You have been told by a doctor that something needs treating first

Coaching assumes a working baseline. If the baseline is not there, coaching asks you to execute things you are not currently able to execute, and that usually makes people feel worse rather than better.

Your doctor is the right route into this, and in most countries they can also start an ADHD assessment at the same time.

## When is coaching the right fit?

When the gap is between knowing and doing. The clearest signal is if this sounds familiar: you know exactly what you should be doing, you have read about it, you may have bought the course, and you still cannot make yourself start.

A business owner put it precisely. He knew what he needed to do. His question was how to take the steps to actually do it.

That is not a therapy question. Nothing from his past was blocking him. What was missing was energy, structure and someone expecting things from him.

## Can I do both?

Yes, and plenty of people do. They do not conflict, because they are working on different layers.

The same is true of medication. Coaching does not replace it and works better alongside it, because medication often makes starting easier and coaching decides what you are starting and whether anyone notices if you do not.

## What should I ask before paying a coach?

Since the field is unregulated, worth asking directly:

1. What is your background and what qualifies you to work on this
2. What happens between sessions, and is there accountability
3. What specifically will be different in twelve weeks
4. Have you worked with people in my situation
5. What would make you tell me I am not a good fit

The last one is the most useful. A coach who has no answer to it will take anyone.

## What does our programme do?

Twelve weeks, built in three stages. Fuel first, which is sleep, energy and environment. Then Focus, which is capture, priorities, starting and finishing. Then Finish, which is handling bad days and keeping it going without us.

Calls every two weeks, with check-ins in between, because the accountability between sessions is doing most of the work.

It is not therapy and it is not treatment. If what you need is treatment, we will say so.

## FAQ

**Is ADHD coaching worth the money?**
It depends entirely on whether your problem is a doing problem. If it is, the accountability is usually what people say made the difference. If your problem is mental health, the money is better spent elsewhere.

**Do I need a diagnosis to get coaching?**
No. The work is on behaviour and systems. Pursue an assessment in parallel if you suspect ADHD, because medication helps many people.

**Can a coach tell me if I have ADHD?**
No. A coach cannot diagnose. Anyone who offers to is not someone to work with.

**What if I have tried coaching before and it did not work?**
Worth asking what was missing. Most of the time it is accountability between sessions, which a lot of coaching does not include.
