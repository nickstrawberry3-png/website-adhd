# Why do I avoid people when I am behind on work?

> **Quick answer:** Because contact means the subject might come up, and the subject carries shame. The avoidance then creates the real damage, because silence is what people actually mind, not the delay. One short message today costs almost nothing and stops the thing that is actually hurting you.


A freelancer we spoke to had four projects outstanding and five people waiting to hire him. He could see the messages arriving and could not open them, because opening one meant facing all of it.

He was losing work he wanted, in a town where he was the only person doing that job, by doing nothing.

## Why does being behind make you disappear?

Three things stack up.

**Contact might raise it.** Any conversation could turn into the question you are dreading, so all conversation becomes risky.

**Opening creates obligation.** An unread message is information. A read one is a reply you owe. So not opening feels like keeping the workload down.

**Shame grows with time.** At three days you owe a reply. At three weeks you owe an apology, and the apology is now the task you are avoiding rather than the work itself.

That last one is why it accelerates. The task gets heavier every day without you touching it.

## What the other person actually experiences

Not what you think.

Most clients and colleagues tolerate a delay they were told about. What they struggle with is silence, where they do not know whether the work is coming, cannot plan, and have to chase.

So the avoidance is not protecting the relationship. It is the thing damaging it. The delay alone usually would not have.

## What to do today

**Send one line.** Not the work. A message. Where things are, and a date you have thought about. No long explanation.

**Pick the easiest one first.** Not the most important, and not the oldest. The point of the first message is to prove it is survivable.

**Use a template for the rest.** Three lines, reused. Most of the paralysis is in composing an apology from scratch each time.

**Give a date you can keep.** Under-promise. You are dealing with someone who has already waited, and a second missed date costs far more than a slow first reply.

| | What avoidance does | What one message does |
|---|---|---|
| Their experience | Uncertainty, chasing | A date they can plan around |
| The task | Grows heavier daily | Stops growing |
| The relationship | Damaged by silence | Usually survives the delay |
| Your position | Dreading contact | One fewer thing to dread |

## What about the very old ones?

Reply anyway. The version in your head where they are furious is almost never the version that exists.

Short, honest, no lengthy explanation, with a date or a clear statement that you cannot take it on. Some will have moved on, which is information rather than failure.

Do these in their own session, separate from current work, and do the easiest first.

## Why does this keep happening?

Because it is driven by the pile, and the pile rebuilds whenever nobody is checking.

The structural fix is a fixed slot two or three times a week for messages, and one person who knows that slot exists. Notifications and reminders have already been tried and the problem was never awareness.

## FAQ

**What do I say about why I went quiet?**
As little as possible. One line. Long explanations make it harder to send, which is part of why it has not been sent.

**What if I have lost the work already?**
Some you will have. Reply anyway, because the relationship often survives even when that job does not.

**Is this avoidance or depression?**
It can be either or both. If you are withdrawing from everything rather than from the work you are behind on, that is worth taking to your doctor.

**How do I stop the pile rebuilding?**
A fixed slot and a person who asks. That is the whole answer, and it is the one most people skip.
