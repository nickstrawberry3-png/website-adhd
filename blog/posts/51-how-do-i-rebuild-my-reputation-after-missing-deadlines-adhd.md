# How do I rebuild my reputation after missing deadlines?

> **Quick answer:** Deliver one thing early to one person. Reputation is rebuilt by a short run of kept promises, not by an explanation. Under-promise deliberately on the next three jobs, communicate before anything slips rather than after, and keep the apologies short because a second missed date undoes everything the apology bought.


This one carries more weight than the practical problem usually deserves. People in this position describe feeling like a liar, or a failure, or like they have lost the thing their work depended on.

The good news is that reputation moves faster than people think, in both directions.

## What actually damages a reputation?

Not lateness by itself. Silence.

Clients tolerate a delay they knew about far more than one they discovered. The damage comes from the period where they did not know what was happening and had to chase.

That matters, because the silence is the part driven by the avoidance. Once a job is late, getting in touch means facing it, so people go quiet at exactly the moment communication would have saved the relationship.

## How do I rebuild it?

**Deliver one thing early.** One job, to one person, ahead of the date. This does more than any message. It replaces the current evidence about you with new evidence.

**Under-promise on the next three.** Quote dates you could hit on a bad week, not a good one. Delivering on time three times in a row changes how someone describes you to other people.

**Contact them before anything slips, not after.** As soon as you know a date is at risk, say so, with a new date. Early notice is treated as professionalism. Late notice is treated as the second failure.

**Keep the apology to one line.** Long apologies make the client manage your feelings, and they are harder to write, which is part of why the message does not get sent.

**Give interim dates from now on.** Something small due early in every project. It keeps the client informed and it gives you the pull that one distant deadline does not.

| | What damages it | What rebuilds it |
|---|---|---|
| Communication | Silence until chased | Contact before anything slips |
| Dates | Optimistic, missed | Conservative, hit |
| Apology | Long, repeated | One line, then delivery |
| Evidence | The late job | Three on-time jobs |
| Structure | One final deadline | Interim dates with something due |

## What do I say to the client I let down badly?

Short, specific, no long explanation, with one concrete thing attached.

Acknowledge it, give a date or deliver something, and stop. Do not promise it will never happen again, because that promise is unverifiable and they have heard it. Let the next three deliveries make the argument instead.

If the relationship is genuinely over, accept that and move on rather than spending months on it. One lost client is survivable. The pattern is the thing to fix.

## What if the reputation damage is in a small industry?

Then it matters more and the same fix applies faster, because word travels in both directions in a small market. The person who delivered early last month gets talked about too.

Pick the most connected client you still have a relationship with and make that the one you deliver early to.

## FAQ

**Should I explain that I have ADHD?**
Usually not to a client. They are buying an outcome. Fix the delivery rather than explaining the cause.

**How long does it take to rebuild?**
Faster than people expect. Three kept promises changes how most clients describe you.

**Should I discount to apologise?**
Rarely. It sets a precedent and it does not address the thing they actually minded, which was not being told.

**What if I am still behind on the work right now?**
Deal with that first, starting with a message today. Nothing about reputation improves while the current job is still silent.
