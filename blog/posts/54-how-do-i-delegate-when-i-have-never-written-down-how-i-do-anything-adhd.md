# How do I delegate when I have never written down how I do anything?

> **Quick answer:** Do not write it down first. Have the person watch you do it once, then have them write the process, then correct their version. Writing a process from memory, in the abstract, with nobody waiting, is one of the hardest possible tasks for an ADHD brain and it is why the delegation never starts.


Every delegation guide tells you to document your processes first. For most people that advice is fine. For an ADHD business owner it is the reason nothing gets delegated.

Writing a process you already know, that nobody is waiting for, with no deadline, is close to the perfect description of a task that will not get started.

## Why is writing processes so hard?

Three reasons, and all of them are structural.

**You already know it, so there is no interest in it.** Documenting something you can do in your sleep offers nothing to engage with.

**It is abstract.** You are describing a thing that is not currently happening, which means reconstructing it from memory rather than observing it.

**It has no end.** Processes can always be more detailed, so the task never resolves. Tasks without a clear end are the ones that stall.

Meanwhile the real process is partly improvised and varies by case, which means the written version is wrong the moment you finish it.

## The method that actually works

**One: they watch you do it, live.** Once, in real time, with them taking notes. You narrate as you go. This costs you one session and it is the only part you have to do.

**Two: they write the process.** Not you. Their version, from their notes, in their words.

**Three: you correct their version.** Reading something and marking what is wrong is a vastly easier task than writing from nothing. This is the whole trick.

**Four: they run it and update it.** The document improves through use rather than through planning.

| | Standard advice | What works |
|---|---|---|
| Who writes it | You, in advance | They do, after watching |
| Your task | Compose from memory | Correct a draft |
| When | Before hiring | After they start |
| Accuracy | Your idealised version | What you actually do |
| Maintenance | Never updated | Updated by whoever uses it |

## What if I cannot even do the watching session?

Record yourself instead. Screen recording or voice notes while you do the task normally, with no editing and no tidying. Hand the recording over.

It feels unprofessional and it works. The person watching will extract a better process from a messy recording than you will write from an empty page.

## What about the tasks that vary every time?

Those are the ones to hand over last, and they are usually fewer than people think.

Split the work honestly into the part that is the same every time and the part that needs judgement. Hand over the repeatable part first. Most owners discover the repeatable part is larger than they assumed, and that it is also the part they avoid.

## What do I do with the finished process?

Keep it somewhere the person using it can edit. A document only you can change goes stale immediately.

And do not try to document everything. One process, in use, that someone follows, is worth more than a complete manual nobody reads.

## FAQ

**What if my way of doing it is not very good?**
Then having it written down is how you find out, and a second person looking at it usually improves it quickly.

**Should I use a process tool or software?**
Whatever they will actually use. A shared document is fine. Choosing software is another project that replaces delegating.

**How long should a process document be?**
Short enough that someone reads it. A page per task is plenty for most things.

**What if they leave and take the knowledge?**
That is the argument for the document existing, which is exactly what this method produces, and sooner than the write-it-first version does.
