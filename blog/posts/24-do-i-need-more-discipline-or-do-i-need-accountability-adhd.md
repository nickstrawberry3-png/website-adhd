# Do I need more discipline or do I need accountability?

> **Quick answer:** For most people with ADHD, accountability. Discipline means generating your own pressure, which is the function that works least reliably with ADHD. Accountability supplies the pressure from outside. If you perform well when someone is waiting and stall when nobody is, that is the answer.


This is the question underneath almost every other one on this site, and the answer is more clear cut than people expect.

Across the people we work with, the single most repeated sentence is some version of: nobody is checking on me.

A salon owner: there are no people here watching you. A photographer: if there is no pressure point, she does not even check the list half the time. A retired engineer: he is just better with outside structure. A resident doctor: she is not accountable to anyone. A consultant: nobody is pressuring her to deliver.

Five people, five countries, five industries, one sentence.

## Why is self-discipline the wrong tool?

Because discipline asks you to generate pressure internally, and internal pressure is precisely what ADHD does not produce reliably.

That is not a metaphor. The reason a deadline works and an intention does not is that one supplies external urgency and the other does not. Asking someone to be more disciplined is asking them to manufacture the thing they cannot manufacture.

It also fails in a particular way. Discipline produces short bursts of overriding, followed by a crash, which is the Burnout Loop. So the more disciplined you try to be, the faster the cycle runs.

## How do I know which one I need?

One test. Think about your performance in two situations.

| Situation | What usually happens |
|---|---|
| Someone is waiting, a deadline is real, a client expects it | You deliver, often very well |
| Nobody is waiting, no deadline, it only matters to you | It does not happen |

If the first row is true and the second is true, you do not have a discipline problem. You have a structure problem, and they need opposite solutions.

Most people with ADHD who have reached adulthood with a job, a business or a degree already have substantial discipline. They have been using it for years. That is usually why they are tired.

## What does accountability actually look like?

Not a reminder. Not an app. A person who notices.

The difference matters. An app that pings you is still internal pressure with extra steps, because nothing happens if you ignore it. A person asks, and something happens if the answer is no, even if that something is only mild embarrassment.

Forms that work, in rough order of effectiveness:

1. Someone who asks about a specific thing on a specific day
2. A standing weekly call where you say what you did
3. Interim deadlines given to a client rather than one final one
4. A group where you state your three things for the week
5. Working alongside someone, physically or on a call

What they share: a real person, a specific commitment, and a time.

One client described the effect in one sentence. She does it because she knows she is going to be asked about it.

## Why is this so hard to arrange for yourself?

Because the people most affected are usually the ones working alone. Self-employed, running their own business, studying for something, building something nobody else is involved in.

That is also why it tends to go unfixed. It does not look like a problem with a solution. It looks like a fact about your situation.

It is not. It is the most changeable part of the whole thing.

## Is this just paying someone to nag you?

Partly, and it is worth being honest about that rather than dressing it up.

The reason it is worth more than it sounds is that the nagging is the mechanism that has been missing for years, and everything else you have tried has been an attempt to replace it with something internal.

## FAQ

**Can a friend do this instead of a coach?**
Yes, and it works if they are consistent. Most people find friends drift after a few weeks, because nothing is at stake for them.

**Does an accountability app work?**
Rarely. The pressure has to come from a person noticing. An app notices nothing.

**What if I find being checked on uncomfortable?**
That discomfort is the mechanism. If it were comfortable it would not work.

**Does this mean I will always need it?**
Some people reduce it over time. Many keep some form of it permanently, which is a reasonable thing to do rather than a failure.
