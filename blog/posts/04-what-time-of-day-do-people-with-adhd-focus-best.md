# What time of day do people with ADHD focus best?

> **Quick answer:** There is no universal best time, but almost everyone has two to three hours a day when focus is genuinely available. We call this the Golden Window. Most people can name theirs within seconds, and most are spending it on other people's work rather than their own.


The advice everyone gets is to wake at five and do deep work before the world wakes up. For a lot of people with ADHD this is terrible advice, because their brain does not switch on until mid morning, and they spend years failing at a routine that was never going to fit.

The useful question is not when should I work. It is when do I already work well.

## What is the Golden Window?

The two to three hours a day when your attention is actually available without forcing it.

It is not the same for everyone. Across our clients it ranges from 6am to around 11pm. One dentist is sharpest straight after early morning tennis. A non-profit VP peaks between nine and twelve and finds one to three very difficult. A freelancer we worked with did his best work late at night and had spent years trying to beat himself into mornings.

The window is fairly stable for each person, and it is usually obvious once you ask.

## How do I find mine?

Ask yourself when you last did good work without having to force it. Most people answer immediately.

If that does not give you an answer, track it for one week. Three times a day, rate your focus out of ten and write the time. A pattern shows up quickly.

Watch out for one trap. Feeling busy is not the same as focusing well. Plenty of people are most active at 4pm and least effective at the same time.

## Why does finding it matter so much?

Because most people are spending it on the wrong things.

The pattern we see constantly: the sharp hours go to email, messages, meetings and other people's problems. Then the person arrives at their own important work with nothing left, concludes they are bad at that work, and avoids it further.

One client described exactly this. She spent her best hours solving other people's problems and reached her own writing with only the dregs of her energy. She was not short of ability. She was spending her fuel in the wrong place.

## What do I do once I know it?

| Step | What it means |
|---|---|
| 1. Name it | Write down the actual hours, for example 9am to 11:30am |
| 2. Clear it | No meetings, no email, no messages inside it |
| 3. Fill it | One task, decided the night before |
| 4. Protect it | Tell the people who interrupt you, so they stop |

Then put the low-value work in the hours you are worst, because those tasks do not need your best attention anyway. Admin at 3pm is fine. Admin at 9am is a waste of the only good hours you get.

## What if my job controls my hours?

Then take what you can. Even protecting the first forty-five minutes of your window makes a difference, and most people can defend that much once they ask.

If your schedule is genuinely fixed, like shift work, the window moves with the shift rather than disappearing. The principle does not change.

## FAQ

**Can your Golden Window change?**
Yes, with sleep, medication timing, illness and age. Re-check it a couple of times a year.

**What if my best hours are at night?**
Then work at night where your life allows it. Forcing a morning routine onto a night brain usually produces neither.

**How long should the window be?**
Two to three hours for most people. Anyone claiming six hours of deep focus a day is describing something other than focus.

**Does exercise move the window?**
For many people it brings it forward or sharpens it. Worth testing, and it varies a lot between people.
