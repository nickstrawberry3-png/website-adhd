# Can ADHD coaching work alongside medication?

> **Quick answer:** Yes, and for most people that combination works better than either alone. Medication often makes starting easier. It does not decide what to start, supply energy you do not have, or provide the external structure that most people are missing. Coaching works on those, and does not replace treatment.


This is general information, not medical advice. Any decision about medication belongs with your prescriber, and nothing here should be used to change what you take.

Almost every client we work with is already on medication and still stuck. That is usually why they come.

## Why do people still struggle on medication?

Because it addresses one part of the problem well and leaves others untouched.

**It often helps you start.** For many people who respond, the gap between deciding and beginning narrows considerably. That is a real and significant effect.

**It does not create energy.** If sleep is broken and you are running on empty, medication acts on a system with nothing in it. One client described needing a medication to take the medication.

**It does not decide what matters.** You can focus beautifully on the wrong task. Several of our clients concentrate well on the work they enjoy while invoices sit unsent.

**It does not supply structure.** On a day with nothing booked and nobody waiting, medication does not provide the pull that was missing.

| | Medication | Coaching |
|---|---|---|
| Starting a task | Often helps | Works on the first step and the trigger |
| Staying on task | Often helps | Work blocks matched to your attention |
| Energy and sleep | Not its job | The first thing addressed |
| Choosing what matters | No | Weekly priorities |
| Someone noticing | No | The core of it |
| Bad weeks | No | A plan written in advance |

## Does coaching interfere with medication?

No. They operate on different things and a coach has no role in your prescribing.

What a reasonable coach should do is notice when something is a medical question and send you back to your doctor rather than offering an opinion. Sleep problems that are not improving, side effects, a dose that stopped working, timing around shift work. All of those are prescriber conversations.

## Why does medication seem to stop working after a few months?

Worth addressing because it comes up often, and the common explanation is not always the right one.

What frequently happens is that the improvement gets used to take on more. The person pushes harder than before and arrives at the same exhaustion from a higher starting point. That is the Burnout Loop with a faster engine. The medication did its job, and the pattern underneath it was never addressed.

Some people do need a review of dose or drug, which is a conversation for the person prescribing it, not for a blog.

## Should I start medication, coaching, or both?

Not a question we can answer for you, and the ordering depends on what is most broken.

If you are not sleeping and running on empty, the foundation matters first, whatever else you do. If you cannot start anything at all despite having tried changing how you work, it is worth speaking with your doctor to review your symptoms and your options. If you can start when someone is waiting and cannot when nobody is, that is a structure problem and medication will not solve it.

Most people we work with end up doing both, and say the combination works better than either did alone.

## FAQ

**Will a coach tell me to stop my medication?**
No reasonable one will. A coach has no business advising on medication, and anyone who does is someone to leave.

**Do I need to be medicated for coaching to work?**
No. Plenty of our clients are not, by choice or because it did not suit them.

**Should I tell my coach what I take?**
Helpful context, particularly around timing and energy through the day, and entirely your choice.

**Can coaching reduce how much medication I need?**
Not a claim we make or that you should accept from anyone. Any change to medication is a decision for you and your prescriber.
