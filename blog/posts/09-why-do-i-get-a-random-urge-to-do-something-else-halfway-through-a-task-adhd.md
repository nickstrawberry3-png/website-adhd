# Why do I get a random urge to do something else halfway through a task?

> **Quick answer:** Because the brain reaches for something more stimulating the moment the current task gets dull, and that urge arrives as a thought that feels urgent and reasonable. It is not a sign you should act on it. Write it on a pad, keep working, and deal with it afterwards. Almost none of them survive the wait.


You are twenty minutes into something and suddenly you need to check a delivery, reply to a message, look up a fact, or reorganise a folder. It does not feel like distraction. It feels like remembering something important.

An hour later the original task is still unfinished and you have no idea how you got to where you are.

## Why do these urges feel so convincing?

Because they arrive dressed as productivity. Checking an invoice, replying to a client, looking something up: these are all real tasks. That is what makes them hard to dismiss.

What is actually happening is simpler. The current task has become unstimulating, and the brain has gone looking for something with more reward in it. The first plausible candidate gets promoted to urgent.

The tell is the timing. The urge almost always arrives at the dull part, not at a random moment.

## What is the Later Pad?

A physical pad, next to you, during work.

When an urge arrives, you write it down in a few words and carry on. That is the whole method.

It works for two reasons. Writing it down satisfies the part of the brain that is afraid of forgetting, which is often what the urgency actually is. And it separates having the thought from acting on it, which are two different things that normally happen as one.

Use paper, not a notes app. Opening the phone to record a distraction is how you lose the next forty minutes.

## What do I do with the list afterwards?

Look at it at the end of the block. You will find three kinds of thing.

| What's on it | What to do |
|---|---|
| Things that were never important | Cross out. Usually the majority |
| Real tasks for later | Move to your actual task list |
| Something genuinely urgent | Do it now, and note how rare this is |

The crossing out matters more than it looks. Watching most of the list turn out to be nothing is what eventually makes the urges easier to ignore, because you learn from your own evidence that they are mostly noise.

## Does this work for every kind of interruption?

No. It works on internally generated urges. External interruptions are a different problem and need a different fix, usually distance and notifications off.

It also does not work if the task is genuinely beyond you right now. If every block on a particular task fills the pad, that is information. The task is probably too big and needs breaking into a smaller first step.

## Why does this happen more when I am tired?

Because resisting an urge costs energy, and by late afternoon there is less available. The same urge that was easy to write down at 10am wins at 4pm.

That is another reason to put demanding work in your Golden Window and leave the dull, interruption-tolerant work for later in the day.

## FAQ

**Is this the same as impulsivity?**
It is related. Impulsivity is acting without a gap between thought and action. The Later Pad manufactures that gap.

**What if I forget something important by not acting on it?**
That is what writing it down prevents. The fear of forgetting is usually the real driver, and the pad removes it.

**Should I use a notes app instead?**
No. Picking up the phone to log a distraction is how the distraction wins.

**What if the urge is to do another work task?**
Still write it down. Switching between two real tasks costs the same as switching to a distraction, and you end up with two unfinished things.
