# Should I tell my employer I have ADHD?

> **Quick answer:** Only when something specific depends on it. Disclose to get an adjustment, not to explain a problem, because an explanation without a change tends to be heard as a prediction that it will continue. Occupational health is usually the safer first conversation, and disclosure cannot be undone.


This is general information, not legal or medical advice. Rights and duties around disclosure differ substantially by country and employer, so get local advice before deciding.

People usually ask this at the worst moment, which is just after something has gone wrong and they are looking for a way to account for it.

## Two different reasons to tell someone

**To get something.** An adjustment, a different way of receiving work, extra time in an assessment. There is a mechanism and a result.

**To explain something.** To account for work that is late. There is no mechanism, and it tends to be received as a reason the problem is permanent.

The first is usually worth doing. The second rarely is, and it is the one people reach for.

## Who to tell, in order

**Occupational health, where it exists.** They can recommend adjustments to your employer without necessarily passing on the diagnosis. Ask at the start what they will share, with whom, and whether you can see the report before it goes.

**Your manager, if you have a specific ask and reasonable grounds to expect it to go well.** Lead with the change, not the condition. Many adjustments need no disclosure at all.

**HR, usually last.** Depends entirely on the organisation.

**A union or professional body, first, if you are unsure.** They see this regularly and will know the local picture, and the conversation costs you nothing.

| | Worth telling | Think carefully |
|---|---|---|
| You want a specific adjustment | Yes | |
| You need exam or assessment arrangements | Yes | |
| Something has just gone wrong and you want to explain | | Wait |
| No specific ask, you just want it known | | What does it achieve |
| Your employer has handled this badly before | | Get advice first |

## What can I ask for without disclosing?

More than people expect. Written briefs instead of verbal. Clear deadlines on open-ended work. Short weekly check-ins. A quieter space. A protected block with no meetings.

Each of those is an ordinary working preference. None of them require you to classify yourself, and they solve most of the practical problem.

## What are the risks?

Honest answer: it depends on the employer, and it cannot be undone.

In many countries ADHD can qualify as a disability with legal protection against discrimination, which is a real safeguard. It is also true that some workplaces handle it badly in ways that are hard to prove. Both things are true at once, and the balance depends on where you work.

Talking to someone who knows your sector before you disclose is worth the delay.

## FAQ

**Do I have to tell my employer?**
Generally no, unless there is a specific safety requirement in your role. Check locally.

**Will it protect me if things go wrong later?**
It can, where the condition qualifies for protection and the employer knew. That is one of the stronger arguments for disclosing early rather than during a problem.

**Should I tell colleagues?**
Separate question. One trusted colleague who puts things in writing rather than saying them in passing is often more useful than a formal process.

**What if I am already in a formal process?**
Get advice before disclosing, from a union or professional body. The timing matters more at that point.
