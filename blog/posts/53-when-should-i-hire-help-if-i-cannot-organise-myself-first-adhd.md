# When should I hire help if I cannot organise myself first?

> **Quick answer:** Now, or close to it. Waiting until your systems are documented is the most common way owners stay stuck for years, because documenting the systems is itself the task you avoid. Hire for the work you reliably do not do, start them on one narrow job, and build the process with them rather than before them.


Almost every owner we work with says a version of the same thing. They would hire, but they need to get organised first. Write the processes down, tidy the systems, work out exactly how they do things, then bring someone in.

That sentence has been true for them for two years.

## Why does waiting not work?

Because documenting your processes is a task with no deadline, no novelty and nobody waiting. It is precisely the shape of task that does not get done.

So the condition for hiring is a task you cannot start, which means the hire never happens, which means you keep doing the work you cannot start either.

There is a second problem. Your processes probably are not as fixed as you think. A lot of what you do exists only as improvisation, and trying to write it down in the abstract is much harder than describing it to a person while you do it.

## What to do instead

**Hire for the thing you reliably avoid, not the thing you are busiest with.** The avoided work is where the money leaks. Invoicing, follow-ups, scheduling, supplier payments.

**Start with one narrow job.** Not an assistant who helps generally. One specific function, a few hours a week. Narrow is easier to hand over and easier to judge.

**Build the process with them.** Have them watch you do it once, then write it themselves, then correct their version. That is far easier than writing it from nothing, and the document is better because it is written by the person who will follow it.

**Accept it will be done differently.** The version in your head does not exist anywhere else. Judge the outcome, not the method.

**Give them authority to chase you.** This is the part owners skip, and it is where most of the value is. A person who asks whether you have approved something is supplying the structure you have been missing.

| | The usual plan | What works |
|---|---|---|
| Prerequisite | Document everything first | Document it with them, after |
| Scope | A general assistant | One narrow function |
| Hiring trigger | When organised | When the avoided work is costing money |
| Process | Written in advance by you | Written by them from watching you |
| Their role | Takes instructions | Also chases you |

## How do I know if I can afford it?

Work out what the avoided work is costing, which most owners have never done.

Unsent invoices. Enquiries not replied to. Follow-ups not made. Late fees. The price increase you have not implemented. Put a number on six months of that. For most people we work with, that number makes the decision obvious.

One client had around five years of unsent billing, somewhere between fifty and seventy-five thousand dollars of work already done. The cost of help was never the issue.

## What if I cannot afford anyone yet?

Then start smaller than a hire. A few hours a month of a bookkeeper. A virtual assistant for one task. Someone who does your invoicing once a fortnight.

The principle is the same. A person attached to the work you avoid is worth more than a system, at almost any scale.

## FAQ

**What should I hand over first?**
Whatever has the clearest right answer and the least judgement. Invoicing and scheduling are usually the easiest first handovers.

**What if I cannot explain how I do things?**
Then have them watch and write it. You do not need to be able to explain it in the abstract, only to do it once while someone takes notes.

**What if they do it wrong?**
Expect that for the first month and correct the output rather than the person. Judge it against the result, not against your mental version.

**Will I just end up managing them badly?**
Possible, which is why one narrow function is the right start. Managing one person on one task is a much smaller ask.
