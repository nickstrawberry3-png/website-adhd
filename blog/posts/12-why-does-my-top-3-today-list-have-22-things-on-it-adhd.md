# Why does my "top 3 today" list have 22 things on it?

> **Quick answer:** Because a list with no removal process only grows. Adding is easy and satisfying, removing feels like failure, so everything stays. A list of 22 gives you no information, so you stop opening it. The fix is a weekly point where things come off the list, not a better app.


One of our clients described her Notion setup precisely. A page called Top 3 Today, which started as three things and had reached twenty-two. A Quick Capture she had not opened in months. A This Week she never looked at.

She had been searching for the right system for two years. The system was not the problem.

## Why do lists always grow?

Three reasons, and all of them are mechanical rather than personal.

**Adding is rewarded.** Writing something down produces a small sense of having dealt with it. It costs nothing and it feels like progress.

**Removing feels like admitting defeat.** Deleting a task you have carried for four months means accepting you were never going to do it. So it stays, and quietly taxes you every time you see it.

**Nothing in the system forces a decision.** Apps are built for capture. Almost none of them make you choose what to drop.

So the list becomes a record of every intention you have ever had, which is a very different object from a plan for today.

## Why is a long list worse than no list?

Because its only job is to tell you what to do next, and at twenty-two items it cannot.

Opening it produces a wall of things you are not doing. That is not neutral. It carries guilt, which makes opening it unpleasant, so you open it less, so it grows further.

Our client said she looked only at the Top 3 section and ignored everything else. She had effectively abandoned the system while still maintaining it.

## What is the Sunday Reset?

A fixed point each week, fifteen to thirty minutes, where things come off the list.

Not a planning session. A removal session. The planning is the small part.

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Read every item on the list, out loud if that helps |
| 2 | Delete anything that has been there over a month and has not moved |
| 3 | Move anything real but not now into a separate Someday list you do not look at daily |
| 4 | Pick three things for the week. Three, not three per day |
| 5 | Pick Step Zero for the first one |

The deleting is the part people skip and it is the part that works. If nothing comes off, you have not done a Sunday Reset, you have done a review.

## Won't I lose something important?

Almost never, and you can test this cheaply.

Keep the deleted items in a separate file for a month. Then look at it. Most people find that nothing they deleted mattered, and the two or three that did came back on their own, because genuinely important things resurface.

That evidence is what makes the next reset easier.

## Why three and not ten?

Because three is a number you can hold in your head without opening anything.

Ten is a list. A list needs consulting, consulting needs opening, and opening is where it falls apart. Three things you remember on your own survive a bad day.

If three feels too few, that is usually the same instinct that produced twenty-two.

## FAQ

**Should I switch to a different app?**
No. Switching apps is a satisfying project that replaces doing the work. Use whatever you have and add the weekly removal.

**What if my job genuinely has twenty-two active tasks?**
Then you have a list of commitments and a plan for today, and they are different documents. The daily three come out of the list, they are not the list.

**Does it have to be Sunday?**
No. It has to be the same time every week, attached to something you already do.

**What if I skip a week?**
Do the next one. A reset done three weeks in four works. One done perfectly for a month and then abandoned does not.
