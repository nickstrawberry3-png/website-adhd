# What should I eat to stop the afternoon crash?

> **Quick answer:** Eat lunch, which a surprising number of people skip, and make it one that does not consist mainly of fast-releasing carbohydrate. The bigger factors are usually not food at all: sleep, skipped meals, dehydration and medication timing. Fix those before optimising the plate.


This is general information, not medical or dietary advice. If you have any condition affecting diet, or you are losing or gaining weight unintentionally, that is a conversation for your own doctor.

The afternoon drop is partly normal. Most people dip in the early afternoon regardless of what they eat. The question is whether yours is a dip or a shutdown.

## What usually causes the shutdown version?

In rough order of how often we see it:

**Not eating.** This is the most common one and the least talked about. People get absorbed, or do not want to interrupt the one good focus window they have had, and reach three o'clock having eaten nothing since the night before.

**Poor or short sleep.** The afternoon is where a bad night shows up. No lunch fixes that.

**A large fast-carbohydrate lunch.** The classic sandwich, crisps and a sweet drink. The dip after that is real.

**Dehydration.** Unglamorous and genuinely common, particularly for people who forget to drink until they notice a headache.

**Medication timing.** The gap between doses, or the end of a dose, often lands in the afternoon. That is a prescriber conversation.

## What actually helps

**Eat lunch, at roughly the same time.** Attach it to something fixed. The regularity matters more than the contents, because the common failure is skipping entirely.

**Include protein and something slower releasing.** Not a diet plan, just not only white bread and sugar. Most people can feel the difference within a week.

**Keep something easy available.** If lunch requires preparation and a decision, it will not happen on a busy day. Something you can eat without thinking is worth more than a better meal you skip.

**Drink water on a schedule rather than when thirsty.** A bottle on the desk you refill at fixed points.

**Put the dull work in the afternoon.** Rather than fighting the dip, plan around it. Admin at three, the demanding work in your Golden Window.

| | What usually happens | What to change |
|---|---|---|
| Lunch | Skipped or very late | Fixed time, attached to something |
| What it is | Fast carbohydrate, bought in a hurry | Protein plus something slower |
| Preparation | Decided in the moment | Something available with no decision |
| Water | When you remember | On a schedule |
| Afternoon work | The hard stuff, badly | The dull stuff, deliberately |

## Do any supplements help?

This is well outside what a coach should be advising on, and most of the claims online are not supported. Deficiencies, including iron and vitamin D, are worth testing for through your doctor rather than guessing at. Ferritin is worth asking about alongside a standard blood count, because iron stores can be low before anaemia shows up. It matters more if you are tired all the time rather than only in the afternoon.

## When is this not about food?

If the afternoon crash is severe, daily, and does not change when you eat properly and sleep seven hours for a fortnight, that is worth taking to your doctor. Thyroid problems, anaemia, sleep apnoea and perimenopause all present exactly like this, and all are worth investigating.

## FAQ

**Does sugar make ADHD worse?**
The evidence for sugar causing ADHD symptoms is weak. A large fast-carbohydrate meal producing an energy dip is a different and more ordinary claim.

**Should I try a specific diet for ADHD?**
There is no diet with good evidence for treating ADHD. Eating regularly matters more than what the eating consists of.

**Why do I forget to eat entirely?**
Absorption in a task, and the hunger signal being easy to miss. A fixed time rather than waiting to feel hungry is the fix.

**Is caffeine making it worse?**
Possibly, particularly if the crash follows a large mid-morning coffee or if it is affecting your sleep.
