# How do I revise for an exam when I cannot make myself start revising?

> **Quick answer:** Make the first action so small it is boring, and attach it to a time rather than a mood. Open the question bank and do one question. Not revise cardiology. Starting is the whole problem, and a session that must become two hours is a session you will keep postponing.


There is a particular kind of day that everyone studying for a big exam knows. You clear the whole day for revision. You wake up early. And at four in the afternoon you have reorganised your notes, watched some videos, bought a new highlighter set, and done no actual revision.

The day was not wasted through laziness. It was spent avoiding the start.

## Why is starting revision so much harder than other tasks?

Four things are true at once, and all four work against you.

**The consequence is months away.** An ADHD brain responds to proximity, not importance. An exam in April produces no pull in January.

**There is no obvious first action.** Revise is not a task, it is a category. Facing a category rather than an action is what stalls people.

**Nobody is waiting.** No colleague, no client, no patient. Nothing happens today if you do not do it.

**It carries dread.** Starting means finding out how much you do not know, and not starting keeps that unknown.

## What is Step Zero for revision?

The first physical action, made small enough that there is nothing to refuse.

| Not Step Zero | Step Zero |
|---|---|
| Revise respiratory | Open the question bank, do one question |
| Go through the lecture notes | Open the folder, open lecture 4 |
| Start the practice paper | Write today's date at the top of a blank page |
| Learn the drug list | Write three drugs from memory |

One question is a complete session. If you carry on, good. If you do not, the material has moved from untouched to started, and tomorrow's session begins from a much lower barrier.

## What makes a session actually happen?

**A fixed time, attached to something.** After dinner. Before the shift. Straight after the gym. Not when I feel ready, because that moment does not reliably arrive.

**The topic decided the night before.** One line on paper. The decision is the part that fails, so make it when you have capacity.

**A floor you can hit on a bad day.** Twenty minutes, or ten questions. Something you would do even after a terrible day. The floor keeps the habit intact when the ambitious version collapses.

**One person who asks.** The exam cannot pull you from four months away. A friend who gets a message every evening can.

## Why does the timetable I made not work?

Because the timetable was made by the enthusiastic version of you and has to be run by the tired version.

Most revision timetables allocate every hour between now and the exam, assume no bad days, and break permanently the first week something goes wrong. Then the broken timetable becomes evidence that you are failing, which makes opening it worse.

A single fixed daily block with a floor survives a bad week. A timetable does not.

## What if I have left it very late?

Then the small daily block matters more, not less, because panic revision is the least efficient kind and you cannot afford waste.

Pick the highest-yield topics, do active questions rather than reading, and tell someone your daily number. Do not build a new timetable. Building it will take an evening you do not have.

## FAQ

**How long should a revision session be with ADHD?**
Start at your Drift Point, which for most people on dry material is fifteen to twenty-five minutes. Two short blocks beat one long one you abandon.

**Should I study in the morning or evening?**
Whichever you will actually do consistently. Consistency matters far more than the hour.

**Is it better to study at home or elsewhere?**
A library or café often works better, because the change of place supplies novelty and other people being present supplies a small amount of observation.

**What if I cannot start even one question?**
Then make it smaller. Open the app. That is a real session, and tomorrow will be easier.
