# How do I retake an exam I have already failed once?

> **Quick answer:** Start with the part you did best in, not the part you failed. The first session has to prove the work is possible, because the material now carries the memory of failing and that is what is stopping you from opening it. Then get the breakdown, work on specifics, and have one person checking daily.


A resit is a different task from a first attempt, and treating it the same way is why so many second attempts go badly.

The material has not changed. What has changed is that opening it now means remembering the result, so avoidance has a layer on it that was not there before.

## Why is a resit harder to start than the first attempt?

Three reasons, and none of them are about the content.

**The material carries the failure.** Every topic is now associated with the outcome. Opening the book is not neutral.

**The deadline pressure that eventually worked last time has already been used.** You know how the panic phase feels and that it was not enough, so the usual last-resort mechanism has lost credibility.

**There is often a time limit or a limited number of attempts.** Which raises the stakes, which raises the avoidance.

So the first obstacle is not knowledge. It is getting the book open at all.

## What to do first

**Get the breakdown if one exists.** Most exams provide some form of performance feedback. Specifics are far easier to work on than everything, and working on everything is what produces the second failure.

**Start with your strongest section.** This is counterintuitive and it matters. The purpose of the first week is to re-establish that you can study and make progress. Starting with your worst topic on day one confirms the dread instead.

**Set a very low floor for the first two weeks.** Ten questions a day. The goal is the habit, not the volume. Volume without a habit is what you did last time.

**Tell one person, with a daily report.** A resit over four months needs a pull that is not the exam, and the exam is now actively unpleasant to think about.

| | First attempt | Resit |
|---|---|---|
| Main obstacle | Starting a big task | Opening material that carries failure |
| Where to start | Logical order | Your strongest section |
| What to work on | The whole syllabus | The specific gaps in your breakdown |
| Early goal | Coverage | Proving the work is possible |
| Pull | The exam | A person, daily |

## How do I work out what actually went wrong?

Separate three possibilities honestly, because they need different fixes.

**You did not cover enough material.** Then the plan is coverage, and the daily block is the mechanism.

**You covered it but could not recall it.** Then the method was passive, and the fix is active retrieval rather than more hours.

**You knew it and the exam went badly.** Timing, nerves, running out of time. Then the fix is timed past papers under exam conditions, not more content.

Most people assume the first and it is often the second. Be honest about which one, because preparing for the wrong problem is the most common reason a resit repeats the result.

## What about the shame?

Worth naming, because it affects the work. Most people retaking something are carrying a version of this that is far heavier than anyone around them realises, and it is the thing driving the avoidance.

The useful move is not to resolve the feeling. It is to get one small session done, because finished work is the only reliable contradiction to it. Three days of sessions does more for how you feel about this than any amount of thinking about it.

## FAQ

**Should I change how I study completely?**
Change the method if recall was the problem. Do not change everything at once, because a full rebuild is another avoidance project.

**How long should I leave before resitting?**
Long enough to prepare properly, short enough that the material is still partly there. Work backwards from a realistic daily block rather than picking the next available date.

**Should I tell people I am resitting?**
At least one person, because you need someone checking. Beyond that it is your call.

**Can I get extra time or adjustments for the resit?**
Worth asking the exam board about, and the paperwork usually takes longer than expected, so ask early.
