# How do I do the admin when I would rather be doing the actual job?

> **Quick answer:** Give the admin a fixed slot outside your best hours, attach a person who asks whether it happened, and batch it so you only pay the cost of starting once. The job you are good at supplies interest, variety and someone waiting. Admin supplies none of those and never will, so the structure has to come from outside it.


A dentist who runs several businesses told us her clinical days run fine and her office days disappear. On days with patients booked she never drifts. On days meant for admin, the morning goes to breakfast and Netflix and it is past one before anything starts.

Same person, same ability, same medication. The difference is whether something outside her is pulling.

## Why does the actual job always win?

| | The work you trained for | The admin around it |
|---|---|---|
| Someone waiting | In front of you | Nobody |
| Variety | Every client is different | Identical every time |
| Feedback | Immediate | None |
| Consequence of delay | Visible today | Invisible for months |
| Interest | It is why you do this | None |

The job arrives with every trigger an ADHD brain uses to start. The admin arrives with none. That is the whole of it, and no amount of knowing the admin matters changes the arithmetic.

## What to do

**Fix the slot, do not leave it free.** Same time each week, attached to something that already happens. A free afternoon for admin is the thing that reliably disappears.

**Put it outside your best hours.** Admin does not need your sharpest attention. Protect the morning for the work that brings money in and put the paperwork at three.

**Attach a person.** Tell someone what you are doing in that slot and let them ask afterwards. This is the single most effective change available, because it supplies the one thing the task is missing.

**Batch it.** Twelve invoices in one session is easier than one invoice twelve times. Starting is the expensive part, so pay it once.

**Make Step Zero opening the folder.** Not do the accounts. Open the folder. That is a complete session.

**Add novelty on purpose.** Do it in a café, standing up, in a different room. On repetitive work this has a larger effect than it should.

## What about the pile I already have?

Do not clear it in one sitting. That plan has already failed.

List everything outstanding on one page, as a session of its own. Then do one item a day, at the same time, easiest first. The first one is not about the volume, it is about proving it can be done.

## Should I just hire someone?

Usually yes, and earlier than most people do. The common pattern is waiting until the systems are documented, which means waiting forever.

The dentist above had been without an admin for months, covering everything herself, waiting until she had written down every process before hiring. The waiting was costing far more than the hire would have. She had not launched the business that would have paid for it.

You do not need perfect systems. You need one person doing the work you reliably avoid. The handover needs its own slot and its own Step Zero, or it joins the pile.

## FAQ

**Why can I do admin for other people but not for myself?**
Because other people's admin has someone waiting. Yours does not. That single difference explains most of it.

**Should I do admin first thing to get it over with?**
Usually not. That spends your best hours on your least valuable work. A fixed slot later, protected, works better.

**How long should the slot be?**
Start at your Drift Point. Twenty focused minutes a week that actually happens beats three hours you cancel.

**What if I skip the slot too?**
Then the person is missing. A slot nobody checks is a plan, and plans are what has already failed.
