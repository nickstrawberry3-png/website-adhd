# How do I know if I have ADHD or if I'm just disorganised?

> **Quick answer:** Disorganisation is a habit. ADHD is a difference in how the brain handles attention, motivation and reward. The clearest sign is not mess, it is the gap between what you want to do and what you can make yourself do. If you can start things you find boring when nothing is forcing you to, you are probably disorganised. If you cannot, and that has been true since childhood, it is worth getting assessed.


Most people who ask this question have already done a quiz online, scored high, and then talked themselves out of it. The quiz told them something they half knew. Then they thought about everyone they know who is also messy, and decided they were being dramatic.

That reasoning has a hole in it. Disorganisation and ADHD look similar from outside. They are completely different from inside.

## What is the actual difference between ADHD and being disorganised?

A disorganised person knows what to do and does not have a system for doing it. Give them a system and they use it.

With ADHD, the system is not the problem. We have tried the systems. We have bought the planner, built the Notion board, set the timers. The problem is that none of it changes whether we can start.

One of our clients, a dentist running three businesses, put it like this: she had a Notion page called Top 3 Today. It had 22 things on it. She had been looking for the right setup for two years.

That is not a person without systems. That is a person whose systems do not reach the thing that is actually broken.

## What does ADHD look like in someone who is successful?

This is where most people get it wrong about themselves. They assume ADHD means failing. Plenty of the people we work with are doing well on paper: doctors, business owners, engineers, people running teams.

Here is what it looks like in them instead:

- They did well at school and university, where someone else set the deadlines, then stalled the moment they had to run themselves
- They can work extremely hard under pressure, then lose days afterwards
- They cannot start anything without a deadline, a crisis, or someone waiting on them
- The work they are good at gets done. The admin around it does not
- They are exhausted on days they barely did anything

One client described it as having an off switch that is simply off. Not depression. Not sadness. Just nothing happening.

## Is it ADHD or is it burnout?

It can be both, and often is. The pattern we see most is called the Burnout Loop: you push hard with systems that do not fit your brain, you crash, you feel exhausted even on days you are not busy, you feel guilty, and you start over.

| | Disorganised | ADHD |
|---|---|---|
| Given a good system | Uses it | Uses it for a few weeks, then stops |
| Boring tasks | Puts them off, then does them | Cannot start them at all without pressure |
| Under a deadline | Works normally | Performs well, then crashes hard |
| Since when | Often recent, or situational | Since childhood, in more than one area |
| After a good day | Feels done | Still feels behind |

The last row matters more than people expect. If you finish a productive day and still feel like you have achieved nothing, that is not an organisation problem.

## Does it matter whether I get a diagnosis?

It matters for two reasons.

The first is practical. A diagnosis opens access to medication and to workplace adjustments, and in some countries to support at university.

The second is the one people underestimate. Almost every client we work with says the same thing after diagnosis: they stopped blaming themselves. A videographer we spoke to had spent years assuming he was lazy, which he knew was not true but could not disprove. After the diagnosis he said the single biggest change was that he finally had a reason that was not his character.

That shift is not cosmetic. People who think they are lazy try harder. Trying harder is what causes the crash.

## What should I do next?

If this sounds like you, speak to your doctor about an assessment. Bring specific examples rather than a quiz score: what you cannot start, how long it has been happening, and what it is costing you at work.

This is general information, not medical advice, and it is not a diagnosis. Your own doctor is the right person to assess you.

## FAQ

**Can you have ADHD and still be successful?**
Yes. Many adults are diagnosed late precisely because they succeeded in structured environments like school and employment. The difficulty often appears when the external structure goes away.

**Can adults be diagnosed with ADHD?**
Yes. Adult ADHD assessment is available in most countries, though waiting times vary a lot. Your GP or primary care doctor is usually the first step.

**Is ADHD just being lazy?**
No. Laziness means not wanting to do something. ADHD means wanting to do it and being unable to start. Most people with ADHD work harder than those around them, which is part of why it leads to burnout.

**Do I need a diagnosis before I get coaching?**
No. Coaching works on the behaviour and the systems, not the diagnosis. But if you suspect ADHD, an assessment is worth pursuing in parallel, because medication helps some people a great deal.

**Does ADHD medication fix the problem?**
It helps many people start tasks, but almost nobody we work with says it is enough on its own. One client described needing a medication to take the medication.
