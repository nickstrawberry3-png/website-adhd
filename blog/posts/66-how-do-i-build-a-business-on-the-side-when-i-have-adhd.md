# How do I build a business on the side when I have ADHD?

> **Quick answer:** Make the unit small enough to survive a bad day at work, protect one weekend morning rather than relying on evenings, and get one person expecting something each week. The job supplies all your structure and the side business supplies none, which is why it stalls while the job keeps running.


This is one of the most common situations we see. Someone capable, holding down a demanding job, with a business idea they have been working on for two years and not launched.

The job always gets done. The business does not. People read that as a lack of commitment, and it is not.

## Why does the job always win?

| | The job | The side business |
|---|---|---|
| Someone expecting things | Constantly | Nobody |
| Deadlines | Real and dated | Self-set, movable |
| Consequence of not doing it | Immediate | None |
| When you do it | Your best hours | Whatever is left |
| Being observed | Yes | No |

Your employer has unintentionally built an excellent ADHD support system. The side business has none of it, and it is also competing for the capacity the job has already used.

That is why the evenings do not work. Not because you are tired in general, but because the specific resource the business needs was spent by five.

## What actually works

**One protected weekend morning.** Same morning, every week, in your best hours. This matters more than any number of weekday evenings. Three hours of real capacity beats eight hours of depleted ones.

**A weekday floor, not a weekday target.** Fifteen minutes on a work night. Small enough to do after a bad day. Its job is to keep the thread, not to produce output.

**One person expecting something each week.** A friend building something, a group, a coach. The business has no deadline, so the person is the deadline.

**Decide the next action the night before.** One line. Not work on the business, which is a category, not a task.

**Cap it at one idea.** The most common failure here is not slow progress, it is three ideas at thirty percent. One. The others go on a list you do not open.

## What about launching?

Launch earlier than feels right, with less than feels right.

A side business that is almost ready for two years is the standard outcome of this pattern, because almost ready has no deadline and launching is the part with no novelty in it. Set a date, tell someone, and ship something incomplete.

The feedback from one real customer will restructure the work more usefully than another six months of building.

## When should I leave the job?

Later than the exciting version of you wants, and the reason is structural rather than financial.

The job is currently supplying your routine, your income and your external structure. Removing all three at once, when the business has not yet developed its own, is the point at which a lot of people with ADHD stall badly.

Build the structure first: fixed hours, a weekly check-in with someone, a defined set of things you do each week. If those exist while you are still employed, the transition is survivable. If they do not, you will discover their absence at the worst moment.

## FAQ

**Should I work on it in the mornings before work?**
Works very well for some people, because capacity is high and nothing has interrupted you yet. Worth testing for two weeks.

**What if I only have evenings?**
Lower the target rather than skipping. Fifteen active minutes keeps the thread, and the thread is what produces results over a year.

**How do I stop switching to a new idea?**
Write the new idea down and do not act on it for a week. Most lose their urgency. The ones that survive are worth considering, after you finish the current one.

**Is it realistic to build something while working full time?**
Yes, slowly, if the weekly unit is consistent. It is not realistic in the bursts most people plan for.
