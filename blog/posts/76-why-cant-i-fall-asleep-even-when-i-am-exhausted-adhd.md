# Why can't I fall asleep even when I am exhausted?

> **Quick answer:** Usually because the evening is the first quiet time you have had, going to bed means stopping something, and delayed sleep timing is common with ADHD so you are not biologically sleepy yet. Fix the wake time first. Bedtime follows wake time, not the other way round.


This is general information, not medical advice. Persistent insomnia is worth taking to your own doctor, particularly if it started or changed with a medication.

The frustrating version of this is being genuinely exhausted all day and then wide awake at midnight. It feels like a contradiction and it is not.

## Why does it happen?

**The evening is the first quiet time.** If your day is interruptions and other people, the hours after everyone is asleep are the first that belong to you. Giving them up feels like a loss, because it is one. This is sometimes called revenge bedtime procrastination and the name is accurate.

**Going to bed means stopping.** A transition, and transitions are the expensive part. Easier to keep doing what you are already doing.

**Delayed sleep timing.** Many people with ADHD genuinely do not feel sleepy until late. That part is biological, not a discipline problem.

**The mind gets loud.** The first moment with no input is the first moment for everything you did not do today to arrive.

**The phone.** Scrolling in bed pushes sleep later without ever feeling like a decision.

## What to change, in order

**Fix the wake time.** Same time every day, two weeks, including weekends. Bedtime follows. Trying to go to bed earlier when you are not tired produces an hour of lying awake and a worse association with the bed.

**Light in the morning, dim in the evening.** Daylight within an hour of waking anchors the clock. Dimmer light for the last hour before bed supports the other end.

**A fixed stop time for work.** This gives the evening a boundary, so the quiet time is deliberate rather than stolen from sleep.

**Phone out of the bedroom.** Removes the scrolling and the overnight checks.

**Get up if you are awake.** If you have been lying there a long time, get up, do something dull in low light, go back when sleepy. Lying in bed awake trains the association you are trying to break.

| | What we do | What works |
|---|---|---|
| The lever | Try to go to bed earlier | Fix the wake time |
| Evening light | Bright, screens | Dimmer for the last hour |
| Quiet time | Taken from sleep | A fixed stop time for work |
| Phone | In bed | Out of the room |
| Lying awake | Stay there, frustrated | Get up, low light, return when sleepy |

## What about the loud mind?

Two things that help more than trying to relax.

Write tomorrow's first task down before bed, along with anything you are worried about forgetting. A lot of the evening noise is the fear of losing something.

And give the day an ending. Two minutes writing what got done and what is next. Without an ending the day does not close, and an unclosed day keeps running in the background.

## When should I see a doctor?

Worth raising if you sleep a full night and wake unrefreshed, if you snore heavily or someone has noticed pauses in your breathing, if you have restless or uncomfortable legs in the evening, or if this started or worsened when a medication changed.

Sleep apnoea, restless legs and thyroid problems all look like this, as does perimenopause. All of them are worth investigating and most have straightforward treatments.

## FAQ

**Is melatonin worth trying?**
Availability and regulation vary by country, and timing matters more than dose for sleep timing problems. A question for your doctor rather than for an article.

**Does ADHD medication cause insomnia?**
It can, particularly later in the day. Timing is a prescriber conversation.

**Why am I most awake at midnight?**
Delayed sleep timing is more common with ADHD, and the evening is also when the external demands finally stop. Both at once.

**Should I just accept being a night owl?**
If your life allows it, that is a reasonable choice. The problem is only when a late sleep time meets an early fixed commitment.
