# How do I ask for workplace adjustments for ADHD?

> **Quick answer:** Lead with the specific change you want, not with the diagnosis. Many useful adjustments can be requested as a working preference with nothing disclosed at all. Where you do disclose, occupational health is usually a better first conversation than your line manager, and ask what they pass on before you say anything.


This is general information, not legal advice. Employment rights and duties vary a great deal by country, so check the position where you work, and a union or professional body is a useful first call.

Most people ask this question with no specific adjustment in mind, which is the main reason the conversation goes nowhere.

## Start with what you actually want

An adjustment request works when it is concrete and operational. Compare these two openings.

I have ADHD and I am finding things difficult.

I work much better from written briefs than verbal ones, could we do that for non-urgent tasks.

The second is a specific change with an obvious answer. It also does not require anyone to classify you.

So the first step is deciding what would actually help. Not in general, for your week.

## Adjustments worth asking for

| Difficulty | The ask |
|---|---|
| Losing verbal instructions | Non-urgent tasks given in writing |
| Open-ended tasks that never start | Clear deadlines rather than when you get to it |
| Falling behind without noticing | Short weekly check-ins rather than long monthly ones |
| Meetings taking the whole day | One protected block, no meetings |
| Noise and interruptions | A quieter space, or agreed headphone time |
| Documentation and admin backlog | Protected time inside the working day |
| Exams or assessments | Extra time, applied for well in advance |

Notice that every one of these is a reasonable operational request that any employer might grant to anyone.

## Do I have to disclose?

Generally not, in most places, unless there is a specific safety requirement in your role. And many of the adjustments above can be requested without naming anything.

Where you do want the protection that a formal adjustment carries, disclosure is usually part of it, and that is worth getting local advice on first.

**Occupational health is usually the better first conversation** where your employer has one. They can assess and recommend without necessarily passing the diagnosis to your manager. Ask at the start of the appointment what they will share, with whom, and whether you can see the report.

## How to have the conversation

**Pick a neutral moment.** Not the day after something went wrong. A request made during a problem reads as an excuse, the same request made in a quiet month reads as professionalism.

**Bring one or two asks, not a list.** A short specific request gets agreed. A long one gets referred to a process.

**Frame it around output.** This change would mean fewer things get missed. That is the argument your manager can act on.

**Get it in writing afterwards.** A short email confirming what was agreed. Verbal agreements fade, and people change jobs.

## FAQ

**Can my employer refuse?**
In many countries there is a duty to make reasonable adjustments where a condition qualifies as a disability, and what counts as reasonable varies. Local advice matters here.

**Will it affect my career?**
Honest answer, it depends on the employer, and that is worth weighing. Requesting operational changes without disclosure avoids most of this.

**Should I tell my manager or HR first?**
Occupational health first where available. Then decide based on what they recommend.

**What if I have already had a poor review?**
Still worth asking, and worth doing promptly. An adjustment put in place now is also evidence you acted on it.
