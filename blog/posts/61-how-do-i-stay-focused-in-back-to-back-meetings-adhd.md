# How do I stay focused in back to back meetings?

> **Quick answer:** You mostly do not, and planning around that works better than trying harder. Write rather than listen, decide in advance what you need from each meeting, and protect a short gap between them. Six hours of continuous meetings exceeds what anyone's attention does, and considerably exceeds an ADHD one.


A day of back to back calls is one of the most common complaints we hear from people in corporate roles. You arrive at five having been present for seven hours and unable to say what was decided in most of them.

Then the actual work starts, with nothing left to do it with.

## Why are meetings so hard?

**They are passive.** Listening without producing anything is close to the hardest attention task there is, and there is no external signal when you drift.

**They are long.** Most meetings run well past anyone's Drift Point, and far past most people's on unstimulating material.

**There is no retrieval.** You hear something once, do not act on it, and are expected to remember it later. That is precisely the weak point.

**They consume the Golden Window.** Meetings are usually scheduled in the morning, which is when your actual work would have happened.

## What actually helps

**Write continuously.** Not full notes. Keywords, actions, anything. Writing keeps attention engaged where listening does not, and gives you a record so you are not relying on recall.

**Decide what you need before each one.** One line, in advance: what I need from this meeting. Then you have a target rather than a stream.

**Capture actions as they are said, not afterwards.** The moment something becomes yours, write it down with a date. Anything left until the end of the day is lost.

**Ask for a gap.** Even five minutes between calls. Use it to write the three things you took from the last one, before the next one overwrites it.

**Decline what you can, and ask for notes on the rest.** A surprising number of meetings can be an update you read. Asking is usually fine and often appreciated.

**Protect one block a day from meetings entirely.** Two hours, in your best window, marked as busy. This is the single highest-value change for most people in this position.

| | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| During | Listening, drifting | Writing continuously |
| Actions | Remembered, then lost | Written with a date as they are said |
| Between meetings | Straight into the next | Five minutes to capture |
| Your best hours | Spent on other people | One protected block |
| Meetings you do not need | Attended anyway | Declined, or notes requested |

## What about video calls specifically?

Harder for most people, because the social signals are weaker and your own device is right there.

Two things help. Close everything except the call and your notes, so there is nothing one click away. And use the camera, not because anyone needs to see you, but because being visible supplies a small amount of the observation that holds attention.

## FAQ

**Is it rude to take notes constantly?**
No, and it usually reads as engaged. If it worries you, say at the start that you take notes to keep track.

**Should I ask for shorter meetings?**
Worth trying. Thirty minutes instead of sixty changes very little about what gets decided.

**What if I genuinely cannot follow what is being said?**
Ask for it in writing afterwards, framed as wanting to get the detail right. Nobody minds this.

**Why am I exhausted after a day of meetings when I did nothing?**
Sustained passive attention is expensive, and with ADHD it is more expensive. The tiredness is real and proportionate to the effort, not to the output.
