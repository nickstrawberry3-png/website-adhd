# How do I run a business with ADHD without burning out?

> **Quick answer:** Treat energy as the resource the business runs on, not time. Protect your two or three best hours for the work that brings in money, give everything else a fixed slot, and get one person checking on you. Running a business removes every external structure you used to have, which is why it burns people out faster than a job does.


A lot of people with ADHD end up self-employed, and the reasons are good ones. Variety, autonomy, work you choose.

What nobody mentions is that going self-employed removes the scaffolding. No manager, no fixed hours, no colleagues noticing, no deadlines you did not set yourself. All of the structure that was quietly holding things together is gone on day one.

## Why does running a business make ADHD harder?

Because the things that used to supply pressure are gone, and the things that replace them are all internal.

| In a job | Running a business |
|---|---|
| A manager expects things | Nobody expects anything |
| Fixed hours | Hours you decide each morning |
| Colleagues notice if you disappear | Nobody notices |
| Deadlines set by others | Deadlines you set and can move |
| Admin done by someone else | All of it is yours |

Every row removes an external trigger and replaces it with a decision. Decisions are the expensive part.

This is why people often do well in employment and struggle a year into self-employment, and conclude their ADHD has worsened. It has not. The support went.

## What should I do first?

Not a new system. Fix the fuel.

**Fixed wake time.** Not an early one, a consistent one. Self-employment makes this slip faster than anything else, and when it slips everything downstream goes with it.

**Protect your Golden Window.** Two to three hours for the work that brings money in. Not email, not admin, not other people's requests. This is the single highest-value change most business owners make.

**Phone out of the room during it.** There is no office norm doing this for you any more.

## Then what?

**Separate the work that makes money from everything else.** Most people avoid the money work and call the avoidance being busy. Sending invoices, following up leads, finishing the deliverable. Those go in the Golden Window. Everything else goes in a slot.

**Fixed slots for admin and messages.** Same times each week, outside your best hours. Decided once rather than daily.

**Three things a week.** Not twenty-one. Decided at your Sunday Reset.

**One person who asks.** This is the one most business owners skip and it is the one that matters most. Another owner, a group, a coach. Someone who knows what your three things are and asks whether they happened.

## How do I avoid the crash?

The crash comes from the Burnout Loop: push hard with the wrong systems, crash, feel exhausted and guilty, start over. In a business it runs faster, because there is nobody to stop you.

Three things that interrupt it.

Write the Bad Week Plan now, while things are fine. Decide the floor you will hold on your worst week.

Build small pressure points rather than one large one. Interim dates with clients beat a single delivery date.

Plan recovery after big pushes before they happen. Block the two days after, and do not fill them.

## When should I hire someone?

Earlier than most people do. The usual pattern is waiting until the systems are perfect, which means never.

A dentist we work with had been without an admin for months, covering it herself, waiting until she had documented everything before hiring. The waiting was costing more than the hire. She had not launched the thing that would have paid for it.

You do not need perfect systems to hire. You need one person doing the work you reliably avoid.

## FAQ

**Is ADHD an advantage in business?**
In parts. Idea generation, risk tolerance and performance under pressure are real strengths. Consistency, admin and follow-through are the costs. Build around both.

**Should I get a business partner for accountability?**
It can work, and it is a large commitment for a function you can get from a weekly call. Try the smaller version first.

**How do I stop starting new ventures?**
Cap the number of open projects at three, and make a new one replace an existing one rather than join it.

**What if my business depends entirely on me?**
Then your energy is the business asset, and protecting it is not self-indulgence, it is maintenance.
