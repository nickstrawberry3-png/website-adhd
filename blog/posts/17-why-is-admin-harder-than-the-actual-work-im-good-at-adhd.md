# Why is admin harder than the actual work I'm good at?

> **Quick answer:** Because the work you are good at has interest, variety and usually someone waiting for it, and admin has none of the three. The skill gap is not real. The trigger gap is. Admin needs a trigger attached deliberately, because it will never generate one by itself.


A dentist told us her office work was the thing she kept delaying, while her clinical days ran fine. A videographer could shoot all day and could not open the editing software. A ballet teacher could teach classes and not do the scheduling.

In every case the person is capable, experienced and well regarded at the main work. The admin around it does not get done.

## Why does the gap exist?

Compare what the two tasks supply.

| | The work you are good at | The admin around it |
|---|---|---|
| Interest | High. It is why you do this | None |
| Variety | Every client, patient or project is different | Identical every time |
| Someone waiting | Yes, usually in front of you | No |
| Feedback | Immediate | None |
| Consequence of delay | Visible today | Invisible until it is large |

The main work arrives with four triggers already attached. Admin arrives with zero. You are not comparing two tasks, you are comparing a task with support and a task without.

## Why does it get worse over time?

Because unfinished admin accumulates, and the pile adds a fifth problem: guilt.

Once a task carries guilt, looking at it is unpleasant, so we look at it less, so it grows. That is how six unsent invoices become sixty, and why the person who could have done it easily in March cannot face it in September.

A dentist described this with her supplier payments. Payroll always went out on time, because people were waiting for it. Everything else did not. Same person, same day, same ability. The only difference was whether someone noticed.

## How do I fix it?

**Give admin a fixed slot, not a free one.** Same time each week. Attached to something that already happens, so it is not a decision. Not in your Golden Window, because admin does not need your best hours.

**Attach a person.** The single most effective change. Tell someone what you are doing in that slot and let them ask afterwards. This supplies the trigger admin will never supply itself.

**Use Step Zero.** Open the folder. Open the file. Not "do the invoicing".

**Add novelty on purpose.** Do it somewhere else. A café, a different room, standing up. On repetitive work this has a bigger effect than it sounds like it should.

**Batch it.** Twelve invoices in one slot is easier than one invoice twelve times, because the start is the expensive part and you only pay it once.

## Should I just hire someone?

Often yes, and it usually pays for itself quickly. Two things to know first.

Handing over still requires a first session to set up, so the handover itself needs a Step Zero and a slot, or it joins the pile.

And you do not need to have your systems perfect before you hire. A lot of people wait for that and never get there. The dentist above had been waiting to fix everything herself before hiring an admin, and the waiting was costing more than the hire would have.

## What if the admin is the business?

Then it is not admin, it is the work, and it needs the same treatment as the work: your Golden Window, a weekly three, and someone expecting it.

Most people in this position have misfiled the task. Invoicing is not admin if invoicing is how the money arrives.

## FAQ

**Why can I do admin for other people but not myself?**
Because other people's admin has a person waiting. Yours does not. That is the whole difference.

**Should I do admin first thing to get it over with?**
Usually not. That spends your best hours on your least valuable work. Put it in a fixed slot later and protect the morning.

**How long should an admin slot be?**
Start at your Drift Point. Twenty focused minutes a week beats a three-hour session you cancel.

**What if I avoid the slot too?**
Then the person is missing. A slot with nobody checking is a plan, and plans are what has already failed.
