# How do I make myself send the invoices I've been sitting on?

> **Quick answer:** Break the invoice into a step so small it is boring, and do only that step. Open the file. Not send the invoice, not do the books. Most unsent invoices are not a money problem or a time problem, they are a starting problem, and starting problems shrink when the first step gets small enough to be dull.


This one is expensive, and most people never say it out loud.

We worked with a retired engineer who had around five years of unsent billing sitting in a drawer. Somewhere between fifty and seventy-five thousand dollars of work he had already done. He was not short of time. He was not bad at the job. He simply could not start.

If you are reading this because you have unsent invoices, you already know this is not about laziness. You know exactly what it is costing you. That knowledge is part of what makes it harder.

## Why are invoices so hard to send when the work is already done?

Because the invoice has none of the things an ADHD brain needs to start. It is not new. It is not urgent until it is a disaster. It is not interesting. And nobody is waiting for it in a way we can feel today.

There is usually a second layer too. Sending an invoice means looking at the thing, and looking at the thing means facing how long it has been sitting there. So the task carries guilt attached to it, and we avoid the guilt rather than the admin.

One of our clients, a dentist, described the same pattern with her supplier payments. Payroll went out on time every month without fail, because someone was waiting for it. Everything else did not, because nobody was.

That is the real pattern: **nobody is checking on us.**

## What is Step Zero and how do I use it on an invoice?

Step Zero is the first action of a task, made small enough to be boring.

Not "do the invoicing". Not "send the invoice". Something closer to:

1. Open the folder where the invoice template is
2. Open the template
3. Type the client's name
4. Stop

That is a complete session. If you carry on, good. If you do not, you have moved the task from untouched to started, and started tasks are much easier to return to than untouched ones.

The usual objection is that this is too small to matter. That is exactly why it works. The brain refuses "send the invoices" because it cannot see the end of it. It will not refuse "open a folder", because there is nothing there to refuse.

## What if there are dozens of them?

Then the size is the thing blocking you, and the order matters.

Do not start with the oldest one. The oldest one carries the most guilt and is usually the most complicated. Start with the most recent and the simplest, because the point of the first one is not the money. It is proving the task is possible.

A reasonable sequence:

1. List every unsent invoice on one page. Just the list, nothing else, as a session of its own
2. Pick the easiest one and send only that one
3. Write it on a Proof List, where you can see it
4. Do one a day, at the same time, in your sharpest hours
5. Do not try to clear the backlog in one sitting, because that is the plan that has already failed several times

## Why does this keep happening even after I clear the backlog?

Because clearing a backlog is a crisis task, and crisis tasks are the ones we are good at. Once the crisis is gone, so is the pressure, and the pile starts again.

The thing that stops it coming back is not a better reminder. It is someone expecting it. A fixed invoicing slot in the week, told to another person, with a check afterwards, works better than any app, because it supplies the one ingredient the task is missing.

One client said it directly: she does the work because she knows she is going to be asked about it.

## FAQ

**Why can't I send an invoice when I need the money?**
Needing the money is a reason, not a trigger. ADHD responds to urgency, novelty, interest and pressure from other people. A distant financial consequence supplies none of those, which is why knowing it matters does not help you start.

**How do I stop feeling guilty about invoices I sent late?**
Send them anyway. Most clients pay a late invoice without comment, and the apology you have been rehearsing is usually longer than the one they expect. The guilt grows every day you do not send it.

**What is the best invoicing app for ADHD?**
Whichever one you already have. Switching apps is a new project and it will take the place of actually invoicing.

**Should I hire someone to do my invoicing?**
Often yes, and it pays for itself fast. But handing it over still needs a first session to set up, so Step Zero applies to the handover too.

**How do I stop the pile building up again?**
A fixed weekly slot in your sharpest hours, and one person who asks you whether it is done. Nothing is checking on you right now, and that is the gap.
