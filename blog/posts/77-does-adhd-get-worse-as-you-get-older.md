# Does ADHD get worse as you get older?

> **Quick answer:** The underlying traits usually change somewhat, with hyperactivity often reducing, but life gets more demanding and the external structure disappears, so many people find it feels worse in their thirties and forties. What has changed is usually the demands, not the condition.


This is general information, not medical advice. If something has changed noticeably, that is worth discussing with your own doctor, because other things can present this way.

People usually ask this when something that used to work has stopped working, and they want to know whether to expect it to keep getting worse.

## What actually changes?

**The traits shift rather than worsen.** Visible hyperactivity commonly reduces with age. The inattentive side, difficulty starting, organising and finishing, tends to persist and is often what people notice most as adults.

**The structure goes away.** School, university and early jobs supply timetables, deadlines and supervision. Each promotion, each move toward self-employment, each step up in autonomy removes some of that scaffolding.

**The demands increase.** A mortgage, children, a team, a business, ageing parents. More things to track, with less external help tracking them.

**The consequences get bigger.** A missed deadline at twenty is awkward. At forty it affects other people's income.

Put together, the condition can be stable while the experience gets considerably harder.

| | Younger | Later |
|---|---|---|
| Hyperactivity | More visible | Often reduced |
| External structure | School, supervision, timetables | Mostly gone |
| Number of things to track | Few | Many, across work and home |
| Consequences of slipping | Small | Significant |
| How it feels | Manageable | Worse, despite similar traits |

## Why do so many people get diagnosed in their thirties and forties?

For exactly this reason. They coped while the structure was there, and the first time it genuinely goes away, usually at a promotion, a move to self-employment, parenthood or working from home, the coping stops working.

That is also why the common self-test of but I managed before is misleading. Managing before says a lot about the environment then.

## What makes it get worse, specifically?

Three things that are within reach.

**Accumulated sleep debt.** Years of short sleep makes everything harder, and it builds quietly.

**The Burnout Loop running for years.** Push hard, crash, feel guilty, start over. Each cycle begins slightly lower, and people describe decades of this before it becomes unmanageable.

**Removing structure faster than you replace it.** The most common one. Nobody builds their own structure until they notice the old one has gone, which is usually a year late.

## Does anything get better?

Several things, and they are worth saying.

People get better at choosing environments that suit them. They stop taking jobs that require sustained attention on dull work. Many build businesses or roles around their strengths rather than fighting their weaknesses.

Self-knowledge helps a great deal, and it mostly arrives with age. Knowing that you cannot start without someone waiting is far more useful than believing you are lazy.

## FAQ

**Does ADHD go away in adulthood?**
For some people the symptoms reduce enough not to cause difficulty. For many it persists and simply looks different.

**Should I get assessed at forty?**
Plenty of people do, and most say the main benefit was no longer blaming their character. It is worth a conversation with your doctor.

**Will medication work as well later in life?**
Many adults respond well. Other health conditions can affect what is suitable, which is a prescriber question.

**Why has it got harder since I started working for myself?**
Because self-employment removes every remaining external structure at once. This is the single most common point at which people find it suddenly much harder.
