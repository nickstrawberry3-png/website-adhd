# How do I stop my ADHD affecting my family?

> **Quick answer:** You cannot remove the effect entirely, and trying to hide it usually costs more than the thing you are hiding. What works is making the unpredictable parts predictable: a fixed time for the things that slip, one shared place where commitments live, and saying when something will happen rather than promising it will.


Most people asking this are carrying more guilt about it than the situation warrants, and the guilt itself is part of what makes it worse.

## What does the family actually experience?

Usually not the thing you think.

It is rarely the forgotten task on its own. It is not knowing whether it will happen, which means they end up tracking it, which is the work you were trying to save them.

A VP we worked with described crashing in the evening with nothing left for her husband, and the sad part was not the chores. It was the creative things they used to do together that stopped happening.

So the two costs are usually: uncertainty, and the version of you that arrives at the end of the day with nothing in reserve.

## What actually reduces it

**Replace promises with times.** I will do it becomes I will do it Thursday evening. A specific time is checkable, which sounds worse and is actually better, because it removes the tracking.

**One shared place for commitments.** A shared calendar or list that both of you can see. The point is not organisation. It is that they can stop holding it in their heads.

**Protect something that is not a chore.** If every bit of remaining energy goes to tasks, the relationship gets only the depleted version of you. One evening or one morning a week that is not admin matters more than clearing the list.

**Do not spend all your best hours at work.** If something at home genuinely matters, give it a weekend morning rather than a Tuesday evening, when there is nothing left.

**Say when you are having a bad week.** Not as an excuse. So that they know the reason, because the alternative is that they invent one.

| | What they experience | What changes it |
|---|---|---|
| Forgotten tasks | Uncertainty, so they track it | A stated time they can check |
| Promises | Heard many times before | One specific commitment |
| Your evenings | Depleted, nothing left | One protected non-chore time |
| Bad weeks | Unexplained change in you | Named in advance |

## What about children?

Two things come up most.

The first is consistency. Children manage an inconsistent parent far better when the inconsistency is named and the important things are externalised. A visible family routine does more than any amount of trying harder.

The second is what they see. Several clients worry about passing on the difficulty. What children mostly take from this is how the adult talks about themselves. A parent who says I find this hard so I use a list models something useful. A parent who calls themselves lazy in front of them models something else.

## What about the guilt?

Worth naming, because for most people it is the biggest part of this and it does not help.

Guilt produces either avoidance or overcorrection, and both make the pattern worse. Overcorrection in particular is the push in push hard, crash, feel guilty, start over.

The more useful response is one change that they can see, kept for a month. That does more for how you feel about this than any amount of resolving to be better.

## FAQ

**Should I tell my children I have ADHD?**
Many people do, in age-appropriate terms, and find it helps. What matters more is how you describe it, as a difference you work with rather than a failing.

**Is ADHD hereditary?**
It runs in families, and a question about your own children is one for your doctor rather than an article.

**How do I stop snapping at them when I am overloaded?**
Usually by addressing the overload rather than the snapping. If it is frequent and distressing, that is worth discussing with a professional.

**My partner does everything. How do I fix that?**
Take one specific thing, give it a fixed time, and do it for a month. One reliable thing beats an offer to do more.
