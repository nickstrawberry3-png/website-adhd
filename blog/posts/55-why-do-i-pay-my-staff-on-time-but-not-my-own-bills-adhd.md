# Why do I pay my staff on time but not my own bills?

> **Quick answer:** Because payroll has people waiting and your own bills do not. Your staff notice on a fixed date and your electricity supplier is an abstraction until it is a problem. The fix is not a reminder, it is making the invisible bills visible and giving them the same kind of external pull payroll already has.


A business owner described this to us precisely. Payroll goes out every month without fail, no issues, never late. Supplier payments, bank transfers, her own bills: hard, delayed, sometimes forgotten.

She was not unable to make payments. She made the hardest one perfectly, every month.

## Why does payroll work and nothing else?

Because payroll has four things nothing else has.

It has a fixed date. It has people who notice immediately. It has a consequence you can feel. And it is the same every month, so it has become automatic.

Your own bills have none of those. The date is flexible until it is not. Nobody notices for weeks. The consequence is a late fee that arrives much later. And every one is slightly different, so none of them become automatic.

This is the same pattern as every other avoided task, just with money attached, which makes it more expensive than most.

## What does this actually cost?

More than people expect, and it is worth adding up once.

Late fees. Interest. Services cut off and reconnected. Suppliers who move you down the priority list. Discounts for early payment that you never get. One client described late fees costing thousands over a year.

None of that is a cash flow problem. It is a starting problem that produces a cash problem.

## What to change

**Automate everything that can be automated.** Direct debits and standing orders for anything fixed. This is not a system, it is removing the task entirely, and it is the highest-value change available.

**Put the variable ones in one fixed slot.** Same day each week or fortnight, attached to something that already happens. Not when they come in.

**Make them visible.** Bills that arrive by email into a general inbox are invisible. A filter, a label, a physical tray, anything that puts them in one place you look at in the slot.

**Attach the slot to payroll.** If payroll is the thing that reliably happens, do the other payments immediately after it. Borrowing the habit works better than building a new one.

**Have someone else see the list.** A bookkeeper, an assistant, a partner. The thing payroll has that the others lack is someone noticing.

| | Payroll | Everything else | Make it like payroll |
|---|---|---|---|
| Date | Fixed | Whenever | Fixed slot |
| Someone notices | Immediately | Not for weeks | Someone sees the list |
| Visibility | Unavoidable | Buried in email | One place you look |
| Repetition | Identical monthly | Different each time | Same process, same day |

## What about the ones already overdue?

Deal with them in a separate session from current ones, easiest first.

Call or email rather than ignoring. Almost every supplier will accept a payment plan or a date, and almost none respond the way the version in your head does. The silence is doing more damage than the lateness.

## FAQ

**Is this a money management problem?**
Usually not. Most people in this pattern have the money and have not sent it. That distinction matters, because the fixes are completely different.

**Should I use an app that reminds me?**
Reminders have already been tried. The problem is not awareness. Automate what you can and put a person on the rest.

**What if my income is irregular?**
Then the slot matters more, because you are also making decisions about timing. A fixed review point beats reacting to whatever arrives.

**Should I hand this to a bookkeeper?**
Often the single best hire for this pattern, and usually cheaper than the late fees it prevents.
