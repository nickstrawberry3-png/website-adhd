# Why does my business stall every time it starts going well?

> **Quick answer:** Because growth is produced by a push, and the push is followed by a crash. The good period uses energy you did not have, the crash takes the following weeks, and by the time you recover the momentum has gone. It is the Burnout Loop with revenue attached, and it is predictable enough to plan around.


The pattern repeats so reliably that owners often describe it as a ceiling. Things pick up, a good month or two, more enquiries than usual. Then something gives, and three months later the business is back where it was.

People read this as a limit on their ability. It is usually a limit on how they are generating the growth.

## What actually causes the stall?

Four steps, in order.

**One: the push.** Something works, so you do more of it. Long hours, high intensity, often genuinely enjoyable at the time.

**Two: the crash.** The intensity was borrowed, and the repayment arrives. Energy drops, starting gets hard, and the things that were working stop being done.

**Three: the backlog.** During the crash, the admin, follow-ups and delivery slip. So the recovery has to clear a backlog before it can produce anything new.

**Four: the restart.** You recover, decide you need to try harder, and push again from a slightly lower baseline.

That is the Burnout Loop operating on a business rather than a person. Push hard with the wrong systems, crash, feel exhausted and guilty, start over.

## Why does the growth itself cause it?

Because growth adds work without adding capacity.

More clients means more delivery, more invoicing, more follow-up and more admin. All of those are the parts you were already avoiding, so a busier business produces a larger pile of avoided work, which is exactly what triggers the crash.

This is why a business can be growing and getting harder at the same time, and why the fix is not more effort.

## What to change

**Build the admin capacity before the growth, not after.** Hire or automate the avoided work while things are quiet. Trying to do it during a busy period does not happen.

**Grow on smaller pressure points.** Interim client dates, weekly check-ins, a fixed slot for the work that brings money in. These produce steady output rather than bursts.

**Plan recovery into the push.** If you know a busy period is coming, block the two weeks after it and do not fill them. The crash arrives either way. Planned, it costs two weeks. Unplanned, it costs a quarter.

**Raise prices instead of volume.** Fewer jobs at a higher price grows revenue without growing the pile of avoided work. This is the most ADHD-friendly way to grow a service business.

**Write the Bad Week Plan while things are good.** Decide now what the floor is when it goes wrong, because you cannot design it mid-crash.

| | How growth usually happens | What holds |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | A push, high intensity | Steady output, smaller pressure points |
| Admin capacity | Added after, if ever | Added before |
| Recovery | Unplanned, long | Planned, short |
| Growth lever | More volume | Higher prices |
| After a crash | Try harder | Return to a pre-written floor |

## Is the ceiling real?

There is a real one, and it is not where people think. It is the point at which the avoided work exceeds what you can clear in a crash recovery.

Past that point the business cannot grow on your effort alone, and the only route through is other people doing the parts you do not do. That is usually a smaller and cheaper step than owners expect.

## FAQ

**Is this just normal business cycles?**
Normal businesses have quiet periods. This pattern is different, because the quiet period is caused by the busy one rather than by demand.

**Should I stop pushing altogether?**
No. Pushes are useful and often how things start. The problem is relying on them as the only mechanism.

**How do I grow without the crash?**
Smaller consistent pressure, capacity added before volume, and recovery planned in. Slower on paper, considerably faster over a year.

**What if I cannot afford help yet?**
Start with automating the fixed payments and getting one person checking on the weekly three. Both are cheap and both reduce the pile.
