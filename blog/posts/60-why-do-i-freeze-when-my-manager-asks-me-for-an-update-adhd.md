# Why do I freeze when my manager asks me for an update?

> **Quick answer:** Because you are being asked to assemble a summary from memory, under time pressure, about work that is partly behind. That is three difficult things at once. The fix is to stop generating the update live and start keeping a running one, so the answer is read rather than reconstructed.


Someone asks how it is going and your mind produces nothing. Or it produces everything at once, unordered, and what comes out is vague in a way that sounds evasive.

Afterwards you can think of exactly what you should have said.

## Why does it happen?

**Assembling a summary from memory is hard.** Working memory is the weak point, and an update requires holding several threads and ordering them, live, while someone waits.

**The question is unbounded.** How is it going could mean five different things. Choosing which one to answer is itself a decision, made under pressure.

**Some of it is behind.** If part of the honest answer is that something has not been started, the freeze is partly avoidance, and that is worth naming rather than explaining away.

**You are being observed.** For anyone with rejection sensitivity, the question carries more weight than it was given, so the physical response arrives before the content does.

## The fix: stop generating it live

**Keep a running update, one place, always current.** Three lines per project: what is done, what is next, what is blocked. Update it as things happen, not when asked. Then the answer to any update question is read, not constructed.

**Have a standard shape ready.** Done, next, blocked. Three parts, same order, every time. A shape removes the decision about where to start.

**Send updates before they are requested.** A short weekly message to your manager changes the dynamic completely. They stop asking, because they already know, and you stop being caught.

**Buy time when caught without it.** Let me give you a proper answer in an hour is a normal professional response and nobody minds it. Freezing and producing something vague is worse.

| | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| When you answer | Live, from memory | Read from a running note |
| Structure | None, so it rambles | Done, next, blocked |
| Who starts it | Your manager, unexpectedly | You, weekly, in writing |
| If caught out | Vague answer now | An hour, then a proper one |

## What if the honest answer is that I have not done it?

Then say that, with a date, and keep it short.

The thing that damages trust is the vague answer that implies progress, followed by the discovery that there was none. A clear I have not started this, I will have it by Thursday is received far better, and it also creates the deadline that gets it done.

Managers deal with late work constantly. What they struggle with is not knowing.

## Does the weekly update actually help?

More than almost anything else at this level. It converts an unpredictable interrogation into a predictable task you prepare once.

It also protects you. A written record of what you flagged and when is the difference between a difficult conversation and an impossible one, if something does go wrong later.

## FAQ

**Is this anxiety or ADHD?**
Often both. The working memory part is ADHD. The physical freeze is closer to the rejection response. Both improve with the running note, because it removes the demand.

**What if my manager asks constantly?**
Usually a signal they do not feel informed. The weekly written update tends to reduce it quickly.

**Should I tell them I need questions in advance?**
You can, framed as a working preference. I give better answers in writing is a reasonable thing to say.

**What if I freeze in meetings generally?**
Same mechanism. Bring three written lines on anything you might be asked about and read from them.
