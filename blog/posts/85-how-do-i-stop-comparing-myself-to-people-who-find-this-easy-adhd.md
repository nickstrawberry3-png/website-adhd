# How do I stop comparing myself to people who find this easy?

> **Quick answer:** You mostly do not stop the comparison, you change what you compare. Measuring your output against theirs ignores that the same result costs you more. Compare against your own record instead, which is the only comparison that tells you anything you can act on.


The particular sting in this one is watching a colleague do effortlessly the thing that takes you three days of dread. Not the big achievements. The ordinary admin.

## Why is the comparison so unfair?

Because it compares outputs and ignores inputs.

Two people send the same report. One opened the file and wrote it. The other spent four days not starting, one evening in panic, and arrived at the same document. The outputs match. The costs do not.

You know your cost and you cannot see theirs. So the comparison always runs in one direction, and it always loses.

This is the same mechanism as feeling like a fraud when praised, pointed outward instead of inward.

## What is actually worth comparing?

Your own record over time. It is the only comparison with information in it.

Did more get finished this month than last. Is the gap between deciding and starting shorter than it was. Are you recovering from a bad week in days rather than weeks.

Those answer a question you can act on. How you compare to a colleague does not, because you cannot change who they are.

| Comparison | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Your output against theirs | Nothing you can act on |
| Your effort against their output | Guaranteed to lose |
| This month against last month | Whether something is working |
| Your recovery time now against six months ago | Whether the foundation is improving |

## What about the things that genuinely are easier for them?

Some things are, and pretending otherwise does not help.

The useful response is to stop competing on those and build around them. If admin is where you lose three days a month, that is a candidate for a fixed slot, a template, or someone else doing it. If starting is the problem, that is a candidate for a person expecting it.

Most of the people we work with have spent years trying to be better at the thing they are worst at, through effort alone. That is expensive and it is what produces the crash.

## What about the strengths?

Worth saying, without it becoming a consolation prize.

The same people who cannot start admin often perform very well under pressure, generate ideas constantly, and do their best work on the things that interest them. Those are real and they are why a lot of our clients are self-employed and successful.

The point is not that it balances out. It is that building around the shape you have works better than trying to be a different shape.

## What helps in the moment?

**Keep the Proof List visible.** When the comparison arrives, you are reasoning from memory, and memory holds the backlog.

**Notice when it happens.** This usually arrives on flat days rather than randomly. If the comparison is loud, check what you were avoiding.

**Reduce the input where you can.** If a particular feed or person reliably triggers this, less exposure is a reasonable answer rather than a weakness.

## FAQ

**Is it useful to compare at all?**
Against your own past, yes. Against other people, rarely, because you never see their cost.

**Should I stop reading about other people's routines?**
If it consistently makes things worse, yes. Most productivity content is written by and for people whose starting problem is different from yours.

**What if I am genuinely behind my peers?**
Then that is worth looking at specifically, with evidence, rather than as a general feeling. Specific gaps have fixes. General feelings do not.

**Does this get better?**
It softens as your own record gets longer and more visible, which is one more reason to keep one.
