# How do people with ADHD cope with night shifts?

> **Quick answer:** Night shifts remove the one thing ADHD relies on most, which is a consistent body clock. The approach that works is to stop trying to flip back and forth and instead protect one anchor sleep block, control light hard at both ends of the shift, and move the demanding tasks to the first half of the night.


This is general information, not medical advice. Shift work affects health in ways worth discussing with your own doctor, particularly if you are on medication.

Most shift work advice is written for people whose attention holds steady when they are tired. With ADHD it does not. Everything that was already difficult, starting, prioritising, remembering, gets harder in the second half of a night shift, and the usual advice to just be disciplined about sleep does not touch it.

## Why are night shifts harder with ADHD?

Three things stack up.

**The body clock stops being an anchor.** Sleep consistency is the single biggest input to ADHD symptoms, and rotating shifts remove it by design. This is not a failure of effort.

**Medication timing gets complicated.** A drug designed around a daytime working pattern does not map onto a night rota, and most people end up guessing. That guessing is a conversation for your prescriber, not something to solve alone.

**The quiet hours are the worst hours.** Between about 3am and 6am there is less happening, fewer people, and less external pull. That is precisely when an ADHD brain has least to run on, and when documentation and admin tend to pile up.

## What actually helps?

**Pick one anchor sleep block and defend it.** Not a perfect schedule. One block you protect on every night-shift day, at the same time, in a dark room. Trying to reset to a day pattern on your days off and back again is the thing that does most damage.

**Control light at both ends.** Bright light early in the shift, dark glasses or minimal light on the journey home, blackout at home. Light is the strongest lever you have over the body clock and it costs nothing.

**Front-load the demanding work.** Notes, checks, anything needing judgement, into the first half of the shift where possible. Leave the mechanical tasks for the hours when your attention is worst.

**Write as you go, not at the end.** Covered properly in the documentation post, and it matters most on nights, because the end of a night shift is the lowest-capacity moment of the week.

| | Usual advice | What works on nights with ADHD |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep | Flip back to days on days off | One protected anchor block, kept consistent |
| Light | Not mentioned | Bright at shift start, dark on the way home |
| Hard tasks | Whenever they come up | First half of the shift |
| Notes | At the end | As you go, in small pieces |
| Caffeine | Whenever you flag | Early in the shift, stopped well before sleep |

## What about caffeine?

Useful early, costly late. The common pattern is a coffee at 4am to get through the last stretch, which then prevents the sleep that would have made the next shift survivable.

A reasonable rule is to stop caffeine at least six hours before your anchor sleep block, and accept that the final hours of the shift will be harder. Six hours sounds excessive until you see the sleep lab data: caffeine taken six hours before bed cut more than an hour off total sleep time, and the people in the study did not notice it had happened. The alternative trades one shift for the next three.

## When should I talk to someone about this?

Worth raising with your own doctor if you are falling asleep driving home, if you are sleeping badly on your days off too, or if your medication timing has never been discussed in the context of shift work. All three are common and all three are fixable.

Worth raising with occupational health if the rota pattern itself is the problem. Some rotas are far harder than others, and a request for a more forward-rotating pattern is a reasonable one to make.

## FAQ

**Can you do shift work with ADHD?**
Many people do, including plenty of nurses and doctors. It is harder, and it needs the light and sleep anchoring to be deliberate rather than left to chance.

**Should I change my medication timing for nights?**
Only with your prescriber. Timing around shift work is a known question and worth asking rather than adjusting yourself.

**Why do I make more mistakes at the end of a night shift?**
Attention and working memory both drop with sleep pressure, and both are already the weak points. Front-loading the work that needs judgement is the main defence.

**Are rotating shifts worse than permanent nights?**
For most people with ADHD, yes, because rotation removes the consistency that symptoms respond to most. Permanent nights at least allow one stable pattern.
