# Why can't I start work in the morning even when my day is empty?

> **Quick answer:** An empty morning removes the one thing that was making you start, which is pressure. With ADHD, a free morning is harder than a busy one, because nothing in it is urgent, new or waiting on you. The fix is not more willpower. It is deciding the first task the night before, so the morning has no decision in it.


The days with nothing booked should be the productive ones. That is the whole reason we clear them.

Instead we sit down at 7:30, decide we will start properly at 9, and look up at 1pm having eaten breakfast twice and watched three episodes of something.

The pattern is so reliable that most of our clients can predict it. They still cannot stop it.

## Why is an empty morning harder than a busy one?

Because a busy morning supplies the thing we are missing.

ADHD does not respond well to importance. It responds to urgency, novelty, interest and other people. A packed clinic, a client waiting, a meeting at nine, these all pull us through the day without us having to generate anything.

Take them away and we are left holding a task that is important, and importance alone has never started anything.

One client, a dentist who runs several businesses, described it exactly. On days with patients booked she never drifts, because the schedule pulls her through. On days with no patients, the same hours disappear. Same person, same energy, same meds. The difference is whether something outside her is pulling.

## What actually starts the drift?

Almost always one thing: the first task was not decided.

We wake up, open the laptop, and the first act of the day is a decision. Which of these nine things do I do. That decision is made at the worst possible moment, before any momentum exists, with every option looking equally unappealing.

So we do the thing that requires no decision at all, which is the phone.

The same client told us she used to plan the night before and always knew her first task. She stopped when she moved house. The drifting started then. Nothing else changed.

## How do I fix it?

Three things, in this order.

**Decide the first task the night before.** One task, written down, specific enough that you could start it without thinking. Not "work on the proposal". Something like "open the proposal doc and write the first heading".

**Protect your Golden Window.** Most people have two to three hours a day when focus is genuinely available. Yours is probably the time you are already wasting. Find it, and put your own work in it rather than other people's.

**Do not let the phone be the first thing.** The First Hour Reset is a fixed wake time, daylight early, and the phone out of reach for the first stretch. If the morning starts with scrolling, the drift has already begun.

| | What we do | What works |
|---|---|---|
| First action | Decide what to do | Already decided last night |
| Best hours | Spent on email and other people | Spent on your own work |
| Phone | First thing we touch | Out of reach |
| Start size | "Work on the project" | One specific small action |

## Why does this keep happening even when I know about it?

Because knowing is not the mechanism. Plenty of our clients can explain their own pattern better than we can and still cannot interrupt it.

The thing that interrupts it is almost never insight. It is a decision made in advance, when energy was available, so that the morning has nothing left to decide.

## FAQ

**Why do I work better under pressure if I have ADHD?**
Pressure supplies urgency, which is one of the few reliable triggers for an ADHD brain. It works, but it burns energy fast, and the crash afterwards is what turns it into the Burnout Loop.

**Is it bad to plan the night before if I am tired in the evening?**
No. It takes about two minutes and you are only choosing one task. Decision quality matters less than the decision existing.

**What if I don't know what my most focused hours are?**
Think about when you last worked well without forcing it. Most people name it instantly. Track it for a week if not.

**Should I just book meetings in the morning to force myself to start?**
It works short term and it costs you your best hours. Better to use external structure on the tasks you avoid, not on the hours you are sharpest.
