# Is ADHD burnout different from normal burnout?

> **Quick answer:** Yes, in cause and in pattern. Normal burnout usually comes from sustained overload. ADHD burnout comes from the cost of forcing a brain through systems that do not fit it, which means it can arrive after an ordinary workload and can keep recurring in cycles rather than once.


This is general information, not medical advice. If you are struggling badly, your own doctor is the right place to start.

The question comes up because the usual burnout advice does not land. Take a holiday, reduce your hours, set boundaries. People try all three, feel better for a fortnight, and end up back where they were.

## What is the difference?

| | Burnout from overload | ADHD burnout |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Too much work for too long | The cost of forcing yourself through work that does not fit how you function |
| Workload needed | High | Can arrive on an ordinary workload |
| Pattern | Builds once, over months | Recurs in cycles, each from a lower baseline |
| Rest | Usually helps | Helps briefly, then the cycle restarts |
| How it feels | Depleted by the work | Depleted by the effort of making yourself do the work |

The last row is the one people recognise. The exhaustion is not proportional to what got done, because most of the energy went into starting, resisting and monitoring rather than producing.

## What does the cycle look like?

Four steps, which we call the Burnout Loop.

1. Push hard with systems that do not fit your brain
2. Crash
3. Feel exhausted and guilty, even on days you are not busy
4. Start over

The fourth step is what makes it different from ordinary burnout. We recover a little, decide the problem was that we were not trying hard enough, and go back to the same method with more effort.

Each round starts lower. People often describe years of this before it becomes unmanageable.

## What does the end stage look like?

Worth naming, because it is where people arrive before asking for help.

A videographer we worked with could no longer open client messages, because opening one meant facing all of it. He had four projects waiting and five clients who wanted to hire him, in a small town where he was the only person doing that work. He had been living off savings for a year with no ability to start.

He was not short of ability or demand. He had pushed through without a diagnosis for years and reached the point where the pushing stopped working.

## Why doesn't rest fix it?

Rest addresses depletion, which is real but is only half of it. The other half is the mismatch between how the person works and the systems they are using, and a holiday does not change that.

So they return rested, use the same methods, and the cycle restarts at the same speed.

What interrupts it is changing the method, which is why energy comes first and systems second, and why there is a Bad Week Plan at the end rather than an assumption that bad weeks stop happening.

## What helps?

In rough order:

1. Stop trying to recover and work at the same time. The first week is about sleep and a fixed wake time, nothing clever
2. Shrink the unit of work until starting is possible again. One small finished thing beats an ambitious plan
3. Get one person checking on you, because self-generated pressure is the thing that has already failed
4. Only then rebuild the systems

## FAQ

**Can you have ADHD burnout without a diagnosis?**
Yes, and it is common. Many people reach this point before ever being assessed, which is often what prompts the assessment.

**Is ADHD burnout the same as depression?**
They overlap and often occur together, but they are not the same. Both are worth discussing with a doctor, particularly if low mood persists.

**How long does recovery take?**
It varies widely. People who have been cycling for years do not recover in a fortnight, and expecting to is part of what restarts the loop.

**Does changing jobs fix it?**
Sometimes briefly. If the method has not changed, the pattern usually reappears in the new job within months.
