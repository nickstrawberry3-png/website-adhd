# How do I work on my own thing after a full day at work?

> **Quick answer:** Lower the target rather than raising the effort. Fifteen active minutes after work is worth more than a planned two hours that does not happen. Do not go home first if you can avoid it, decide the task the night before, and treat weekend mornings as where the real volume goes.


The plan is always the same. Work until six, home by seven, two solid hours on your own thing before bed.

It happens twice. Then it stops, and the stopping gets interpreted as not wanting it enough.

## Why do evenings fail?

**The capacity has been spent.** Not general tiredness. The specific resource that starting a task requires has been used at work, and it does not replenish by seven.

**Getting home is a transition.** Stopping one mode and starting another costs more with ADHD than either mode does. Once you have sat down at home, the cost roughly doubles.

**Nothing is waiting.** Your own thing has no deadline and nobody expecting it, which is already the hardest shape of task, now attempted at the worst time of day.

**The target is wrong.** Two hours is a plan for someone who is fresh. Planning for your best state guarantees failure on an average day.

## What works

**Do not go home first.** Stop somewhere on the way. A café, a library, a desk somewhere. This single change does more than anything else on this list, because it removes the transition and supplies a little novelty and observation.

**Fifteen minutes is the target, not the minimum.** A real fifteen minutes beats a planned two hours that does not happen. If it becomes forty, good. Fifteen is the commitment.

**Decide the task the night before.** One line, specific enough to start without thinking. Not work on the website. Write the three page titles.

**Weekend mornings carry the volume.** One protected morning in your best hours. That is where real progress happens. Weekdays keep the thread alive between them.

**Phone in another room.** The evening session is the most vulnerable of the week, because resistance is cheapest to give in to when you are already tired.

| | The usual plan | What works |
|---|---|---|
| Weekday target | Two hours | Fifteen minutes |
| Where | At home, after dinner | On the way home |
| Task chosen | Decided in the moment | Written last night |
| Real volume | Weekday evenings | One weekend morning |
| If you skip a day | Plan is broken | Do the next one |

## What if some evenings I genuinely have nothing left?

Then take the floor down to five minutes rather than skipping. Open the file, do one small thing, close it.

The reason this matters is not output. It is that an unbroken run is far easier to continue than a restarted one, and the restart is where most side projects die.

## Should I use annual leave for it?

It works well, with one caveat. A full day off for your own project usually produces a drifting day unless it is structured, because it has the same shape as every other empty day.

Decide the three things the night before, start in your best hours, and tell someone what you will have done by the end of it.

## FAQ

**Is it better to get up early instead?**
For many people yes, because capacity is highest and nothing has interrupted you. Test it for two weeks before deciding.

**What if my job is physically exhausting?**
Then weekday evenings are probably not available at all, and the weekend morning becomes the whole plan. That is workable.

**How long until I see progress?**
Slower than the burst version promises and faster than most people expect, because the burst version produces nothing most weeks.

**Should I tell my employer about the side project?**
Depends on your contract and industry. Check it, and keep the two entirely separate in terms of time and equipment.
