# Why do I start a new project every time the current one gets boring?

> **Quick answer:** Because at the exact moment a project loses its novelty, a new idea arrives carrying everything the current one has lost. It is not a fair comparison and losing it is not a discipline failure. Cap the number of open projects, make a new one replace an existing one, and write ideas down without acting on them.


The timing is what gives this away. The switch almost never happens at the start of a project and almost never at the end. It happens in the middle, where the interesting part has finished and the finishing work has begun.

A software developer we spoke to was running four projects and completing none. A publisher had more ideas than he could count and nothing shipped. Same point in the cycle, every time.

## Why does the new idea always win?

| The current project, mid-way | A new idea |
|---|---|
| Familiar | Novel |
| Next step unclear and dull | First step obvious and exciting |
| Carries a record of being behind | No history at all |
| What is left is finishing | What is there is starting |

Every row favours the new thing on the one axis an ADHD brain uses to allocate attention. So the outcome is not a choice you are making badly. It is the predictable result of that comparison.

## What stops it

**Cap open projects at three.** Three in existence, not three a day. Everything else goes on a list.

**One in, one out.** A new project can start, and it has to replace an existing one. Replacing means finishing it or killing it in writing. Killing deliberately is fine and much better than leaving it to rot.

**Write ideas down and leave them a week.** Capture removes most of the urgency, which is usually the real driver. The ones still interesting after seven days are worth looking at.

**Name what finishing means, at the start.** Four or five checkable items, written while you are excited, because that is the only time you will write them. Without it there is no end condition and the project cannot be finished by definition.

**Keep a Proof List.** Finished work disappears and unfinished work stays visible, so people in this pattern have a badly distorted view of their own output.

## What if the new idea really is better?

It might be. Then kill the old one properly, in writing, and say so to anyone involved.

The damage is not from changing direction. It is from accumulating silent half-finished things, each of which carries a small ongoing cost in guilt and attention.

## Why does finishing feel like nothing?

Because for many people with ADHD, completion produces very little reward. One client described finishing a major piece of work and feeling nothing at all.

If finishing does not pay and starting does, the brain allocates accordingly. That is the engine of this whole pattern.

The partial fix is external. Write finished things down where you can see them, and tell a person when you deliver. The reward your brain does not generate can be supplied from outside.

## FAQ

**Is having lots of ideas a problem?**
No, it is an advantage, and most of our clients have it. The ratio of started to finished is the problem.

**How do I finish something I am bored of?**
Attach a person with a date. Novelty is gone and cannot be recovered, so the pull has to come from somewhere else.

**What if I already have twelve half-finished things?**
Finish one, kill the rest in writing. Do not attempt to rescue them all, because the rescue plan is another new project.

**Should I tell people when I abandon something?**
If anyone was involved or waiting, yes, and briefly. Silent abandonment is what damages relationships, not the decision.
