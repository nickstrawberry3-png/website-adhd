# How do I get back on track after a bad week?

> **Quick answer:** Do not restart. Restarting means rebuilding everything at once, which fails, which produces another bad week. Pick one thing, usually the wake time, do only that for a week, and leave everything else broken. A Bad Week Plan is what stops a bad week becoming a bad quarter.


Bad weeks happen to everyone. What makes ADHD different is what happens after one.

The usual response is to treat Monday as a fresh start and reinstate everything simultaneously: the wake time, the morning routine, the gym, the task list, the phone rules. All of it, on day one, on low energy.

By Wednesday most of it is gone, and now there are two bad weeks.

## Why does restarting fail?

Because it requires the most energy at the moment you have the least.

Every habit has a cost to run. Reinstating six at once means paying six costs on a week you are already depleted. The failure is arithmetic, not weakness.

There is also a second problem. A full restart frames the previous week as a total loss, which adds guilt, and guilt reduces the chance of doing anything at all.

## What is the Bad Week Plan?

A plan written in advance, while things are going well, for what you do when they are not. Written in advance because you cannot design it during a bad week.

It has three parts.

**One thing, not everything.** Pick the single habit that most affects the others. For almost everyone this is the wake time, because it determines sleep, energy and the whole shape of the day. Do that one. Let the rest stay broken for now.

**A floor, not a target.** Decide the minimum version of your work that still counts. One task a day. Twenty minutes. Whatever is small enough that you will do it on the worst day. The floor is what keeps the thread intact.

**Someone who knows.** Tell one person you are having a bad week. Not for sympathy. So that someone is still expecting something from you, because the absence of that is usually what turned a bad day into a bad week.

| | Full restart | Bad Week Plan |
|---|---|---|
| How many habits | All of them | One |
| Standard | Back to normal immediately | A floor you can hit on the worst day |
| Framing | Last week was a write-off | Last week happened, the thread continues |
| Usual outcome | Collapses by Wednesday | Holds, slowly rebuilds |

## What order do things come back?

Sleep first, always. Then one work block a day at your Drift Point length. Then your weekly three. Then everything else.

Roughly a week per layer. Faster than that is the restart pattern wearing a different name.

## Why does this matter more than it sounds?

Because for most of the people we work with, the bad week is not the problem. The response to the bad week is.

One week off is survivable. The pattern where one week off becomes a month, because the restart keeps failing and each failure makes the next attempt heavier, is what people are actually describing when they say they cannot stay consistent.

The Bad Week Plan exists so there is nothing to restart.

## What if the bad week was caused by something real?

Illness, a family situation, a crisis at work. Then the plan is the same and the floor should be lower.

What does not help is treating a bad week caused by real events as evidence that your systems do not work. They did not fail. They were not the thing being tested.

## FAQ

**How do I know if it is a bad week or burnout?**
A bad week recovers with sleep and a lower floor. If flat weeks keep arriving regardless of rest, that is the Burnout Loop and it needs a different approach.

**Should I write the plan now even though things are fine?**
That is the only time you can write it. Trying to design it mid-week does not work.

**What if I have been off track for months, not a week?**
Same method, slower. One habit, lowest possible floor, one person. Do not attempt the full rebuild.

**Does missing a day mean the week is ruined?**
No, and the belief that it does is what turns one missed day into five.
